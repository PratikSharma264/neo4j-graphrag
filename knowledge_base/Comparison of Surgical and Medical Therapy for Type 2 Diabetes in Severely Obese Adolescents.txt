"Comparison of Surgical and Medical Therapy for Type 2 Diabetes in Severely Obese Adolescents" evaluates the two-year outcomes of glycemic control, body mass index, blood pressure, lipid profiles, kidney function, and clinical adverse events in severely obese adolescents with type 2 diabetes undergoing bariatric surgery compared to those receiving medical therapy. The study found that surgical intervention led to a significant decrease in mean hemoglobin A1c concentration from 6.8% to 5.5% and a 29% reduction in body mass index, accompanied by marked improvements in blood pressure, dyslipidemia, and kidney function, whereas medical therapy resulted in increased hemoglobin A1c from 6.4% to 7.8%, a 3.7% increase in body mass index, and worsening or unchanged risk factors, though 23% of surgical patients required subsequent operations for complications. Based on these findings, the paper recommends conducting a well-designed, prospective controlled study to further define the precise role, long-term health, and surgical outcomes of metabolic surgery for adolescents with type 2 diabetes.
