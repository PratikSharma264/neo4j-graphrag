The study  “Diabetic Ketoacidosis Among Obese African-American Adolescents With NIDDM” evaluates whether ketosis occurs at presentation among islet cell antibody (ICA)-negative African-American adolescents diagnosed with non-insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus (NIDDM) by reviewing patient charts at Children's Hospital Medical Center from 1982 to 1995. The study's key findings reveal that 42% of ICA-negative African-American adolescents with NIDDM presented with ketonuria and 25% presented with diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), whereas none of the white adolescent patients displayed ketonuria at presentation or follow-up, demonstrating that presentation with ketosis or DKA in obese young African-American patients does not automatically imply insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus (IDDM) or lifelong insulin dependence. Based on these findings, the authors recommend that clinicians carefully evaluate multiple clinical features at presentation rather than relying solely on the presence of ketosis to make a diagnosis, reevaluate provisional diagnoses over time, consider treatment options beyond sole insulin reliance (including diet, exercise, and oral hypoglycemic agents) to tailor therapy to individual physiology and avoid complications like weight gain, and conduct diabetes screening among high-risk obese black adolescents—particularly those with a strong family history of NIDDM—to prevent severe metabolic deterioration.
