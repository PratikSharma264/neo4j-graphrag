The study “Screening eye exams in youth with type 1 diabetes under 18 years of age: once may be enough?” evaluates the optimal frequency and necessity of eye screening examinations in pediatric patients under 18 years of age with type 1 diabetes, drawing on long-term data from the Diabetes Control and Complications Trial (DCCT) and the Epidemiology of Diabetes Interventions and Complications (EDIC) research group. The authors analyzed retinopathy progression in youth to determine whether repeated annual eye exams in early adolescence provide significant clinical benefit or if less frequent screening is sufficient. The findings demonstrate that retinopathy in youth under 18 with type 1 diabetes is exceedingly rare and generally mild when present, suggesting that frequent annual eye examinations in this younger demographic may offer minimal added value. Based on these findings, the study provides recommendations and guidelines suggesting that eye screening once during adolescence—rather than annual exams starting shortly after diagnosis—may be adequate for low-risk youth with type 1 diabetes until they reach adulthood or develop longer diabetes duration.
