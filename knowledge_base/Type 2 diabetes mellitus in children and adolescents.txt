"Type 2 diabetes mellitus in children and adolescents" evaluates the rising global incidence, pathophysiology, clinical presentation, differential diagnosis, and management of type 2 diabetes mellitus in youth. The review finds that pediatric type 2 diabetes is strongly linked to the childhood obesity epidemic, peak incidence during puberty, and higher prevalence in specific high-risk ethnic groups (such as African-American, Hispanic, Asian/Pacific Islander, and Native American populations), with many cases presenting asymptomatically or mild in severity. Based on these findings, the paper recommends targeted screening for overweight or obese youth aged 10 and older (or at pubertal onset) who possess additional risk factors like a family history of diabetes or signs of insulin resistance, utilizing fasting glucose, oral glucose tolerance testing, or HbA1c every two years. Finally, the study guidelines emphasize lifestyle intervention (diet and exercise) as the primary first-line treatment, supplemented by pharmacological therapy such as metformin when lifestyle modifications alone are insufficient, while highlighting the need to restructure healthcare delivery to improve long-term retention and care adherence among affected adolescents.
