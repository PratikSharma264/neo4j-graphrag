Care of Young Children With Diabetes in the Childcare and Community Setting: A Statement of the American Diabetes Association evaluates the unique diabetes management needs, developmental factors, and legal protections for young children (aged 5 years and under) with diabetes—primarily type 1—within childcare and community settings. The paper finds that type 1 diabetes incidence is rising in young children who depend entirely on adults for daily care, while also noting that many families face unlawful discrimination, such as daycare refusal or expulsion due to blanket "no medication" policies. To address these challenges, the document outlines guidelines and recommendations centered on proactive, individualized care plans, stating that childcare programs are covered by federal antidiscrimination laws (such as the Americans with Disabilities Act) and must provide reasonable accommodations—including having trained, non-clinical staff available to monitor glucose levels, administer insulin and glucagon, and manage hyper- or hypoglycemia—while recommending a target HbA1c of <7% (or 7.5% for very young children) alongside collaborative partnerships between healthcare providers, parents, and childcare staff to ensure safe transitions and optimal health outcomes.
