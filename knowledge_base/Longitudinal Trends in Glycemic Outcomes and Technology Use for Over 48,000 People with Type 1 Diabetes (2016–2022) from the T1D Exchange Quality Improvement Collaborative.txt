Longitudinal Trends in Glycemic Outcomes and Technology Use for Over 48,000 People with Type 1 Diabetes (2016–2022) from the T1D Exchange Quality Improvement Collaborative evaluates longitudinal trends in glycemic control (HbA1c) and diabetes technology adoption across more than 48,000 individuals with type 1 diabetes, stratified by race/ethnicity and insurance status. The findings show an overall clinically significant improvement in average HbA1c levels from 8.7% in 2016–2017 to 8.4% in 2021–2022, accompanied by a rise in participants reaching target HbA1c levels (<7%) from 20% to 26% and a decrease in those with severe hyperglycemia (>9%) from 43% to 31%. Alongside these glycemic gains, the uptake of diabetes technology increased significantly across continuous glucose monitors, insulin pumps, and hybrid closed-loop systems; however, pronounced health inequities persisted, as non-Hispanic White and privately insured individuals consistently maintained better glycemic outcomes and higher device adoption rates than non-Hispanic Black, Hispanic, and publicly insured patients. Based on these insights, the study highlights the need for targeted quality improvement interventions, policy adjustments, and funded research to directly address socio-behavioral barriers and ensure equitable access to diabetes technology and care across diverse populations.
