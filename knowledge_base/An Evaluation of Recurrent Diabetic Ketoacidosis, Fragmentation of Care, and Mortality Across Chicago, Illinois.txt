"An Evaluation of Recurrent Diabetic Ketoacidosis, Fragmentation of Care, and Mortality Across Chicago, Illinois" evaluates recurrent diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), healthcare fragmentation, and all-cause mortality using a deidentified EHR dataset from six Chicago-area institutions covering 3,615 patients with DKA between 2006 and 2012. The study found that 21.6% of patients experienced recurrent DKA, with those having four or more episodes accounting for 26.3% of total encounters, and 16% of recurrent patients experienced fragmented care across multiple hospitals, which was significantly associated with higher recurrence rates and increased mean HbA1c levels. Furthermore, 13.6% of patients died during the study period, with mortality significantly associated with increasing age and a higher number of DKA encounters, though care fragmentation itself was not an independent factor in mortality. The authors conclude that recurrent DKA is a common, high-risk, and potentially avoidable challenge heavily linked to healthcare fragmentation and mortality, and they recommend that future public health efforts and research focus on early identification, tracking, and improved continuity of care for this unique patient population.
