The study “Excess BMI in Childhood: A Modifiable Risk Factor for Type 1 Diabetes Development?” evaluates the longitudinal effect of cumulative excess body mass index (ceBMI)—a measure tracking persistent BMI elevation above the 85th percentile over time—on the progression to type 1 diabetes among 1,117 autoantibody-positive pediatric relatives within the TrialNet Pathway to Prevention cohort. The study finds that higher ceBMI is associated with a significantly greater risk of type 1 diabetes progression (a 6.3% increased relative risk per 1kg/m² increase in ceBMI and a 63% increased risk for those persistently overweight or obese), with younger children (<12 years old) and females exhibiting lower risk thresholds and greater sensitivity to body weight compared to older children and males. Based on these findings, the paper suggests that lifestyle modifications aimed at managing weight may help delay the onset of type 1 diabetes in high-risk pediatric populations and highlights the need to use age- and sex-specific ceBMI thresholds rather than standard BMI percentiles when implementing such interventions.
