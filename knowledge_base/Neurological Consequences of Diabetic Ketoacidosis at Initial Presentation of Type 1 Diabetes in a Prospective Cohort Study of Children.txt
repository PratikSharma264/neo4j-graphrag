The study Neurological Consequences of Diabetic Ketoacidosis at Initial Presentation of Type 1 Diabetes in a Prospective Cohort Study of Children evaluates the acute and medium-term impacts of new-onset diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) on brain morphology and cognitive function in children newly diagnosed with Type 1 Diabetes. By assessing children aged 6 to 18 years at baseline, day 5, day 28, and 6 months using MRI, MRS, and cognitive testing, the study highlights how DKA alters cerebral structure and neurocognitive performance even without the presence of severe clinical cerebral edema. The findings reveal that children presenting with DKA experience significant changes in white and gray matter structures, including higher total white matter volume and increased mean diffusivity in the frontal, temporal, and parietal lobes at presentation, alongside reduced total cortical gray matter volume and lower levels of N-acetylaspartate in the frontal gray matter and basal ganglia. Although acute structural and volume changes resolved over time, DKA presentation was associated with persistent adverse functional outcomes at the 6-month follow-up, specifically lower delayed memory recall, poorer sustained and divided attention, and reduced baseline mental state scores. Furthermore, lower blood pH at presentation and younger age were identified as key predictors of both structural brain changes and poorer neurocognitive outcomes. The recommendations and guidelines stemming from this study emphasize the critical need to view DKA-related brain injury along a continuous spectrum rather than as an isolated risk limited to overt cerebral edema. Clinicians and healthcare providers are urged to prioritize early detection and prompt intervention for Type 1 Diabetes in pediatric patients to prevent initial DKA episodes entirely, thereby protecting vulnerable developing brain tissue.
