"Surgery for the treatment of obesity in children and adolescents" evaluates the effectiveness and safety of surgical interventions compared to non-surgical lifestyle programs for managing obesity in children and adolescents under 18 years of age. Based on a single randomized controlled trial involving 50 Australian adolescents over two years, the study found that laparoscopic adjustable gastric banding resulted in a significantly greater mean weight reduction of 34.6 kg (a BMI change of 12.7) compared to a 3.0 kg reduction (a BMI change of 1.3) in the lifestyle intervention group, alongside minor improvements in physical functioning and health perception quality-of-life scores. However, adverse events were frequent, occurring in 48% of the surgical group—with 28% requiring revisional surgery due to complications—compared to 44% in the lifestyle group. The authors conclude that there is currently insufficient low-bias evidence to form definitive clinical guidelines, recommending that future research conduct larger high-quality randomized controlled trials with long-term follow-up into adulthood to properly assess efficacy across diverse populations and identify post-operative care strategies that minimize complications and revision rates.
