"Dietary Protein Requirements in Children: Methods for Consideration" evaluates the current methodologies used to determine dietary protein recommendations in children, assessing the limitations of traditional nitrogen balance studies alongside newer approaches such as the indicator amino acid oxidation (IAAO) technique, 15N end-product method, virtual biopsy approach, and 2H3-creatine dilution method, while also examining physical activity as a potential modifier of these needs. The authors find that standard nitrogen balance techniques systematically underestimate protein requirements due to methodological errors, whereas alternative stable isotope methods (like IAAO) suggest actual requirements may be up to 60% higher than existing guidelines. Furthermore, evidence indicates that engaging in moderate-to-high levels of physical activity increases lean body mass and protein turnover, likely elevating protein needs to support exercise adaptations and tissue deposition; however, existing research remains insufficient to establish separate guidelines based on activity levels. The paper notes that current Dietary Reference Intakes (DRIs) recommend 0.95 g·kg⁻¹·day⁻¹ for children aged 4–13 years and 0.85 g·kg⁻¹·day⁻¹ for ages 14–18 years based on nitrogen balance, concluding that while available data are not yet robust enough to officially overturn these baseline standards, further rigorous research using advanced tracer techniques is urgently needed to accurately define age- and activity-specific protein requirements.
