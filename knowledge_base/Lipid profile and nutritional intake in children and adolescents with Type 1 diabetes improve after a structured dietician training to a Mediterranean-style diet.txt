“Lipid profile and nutritional intake in children and adolescents with Type 1 diabetes improve after a structured dietician training to a Mediterranean-style diet” evaluates nutritional intakes and lipid profiles in 96 children and adolescents with Type 1 diabetes before and after a six-month structured dietician training program to a Mediterranean-style diet. The study finds that at baseline, participants consumed a generally balanced diet but had excessive fat intake and low dietary fiber intake, resulting in a high rate of abnormal lipid profiles where 64.5% and 29.1% of subjects exceeded the 75th and 95th percentiles for lipid risk parameters, respectively; following the six-month intervention, daily fat and cholesterol intake significantly decreased while fiber intake increased, leading to statistically significant reductions in LDL-cholesterol, non-HDL-cholesterol, and total cholesterol to HDL-cholesterol ratios independently of body weight and glycemic control (HbA_1c). Based on these findings, the paper recommends incorporating structured, dietician-led nutritional training focused on a Mediterranean-style diet into standard pediatric diabetes management to optimize dietary quality and lower long-term cardiovascular risk factors.
