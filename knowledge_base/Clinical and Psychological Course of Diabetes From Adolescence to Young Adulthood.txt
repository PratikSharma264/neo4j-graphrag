"Clinical and Psychological Course of Diabetes From Adolescence to Young Adulthood" evaluates the longitudinal clinical and psychological progress of 76 adolescents with type 1 diabetes over an 8-year period into young adulthood to determine the relationship between adolescent psychological state and later diabetic outcomes. The study's key findings reveal that glycemic control peaked and worsened during late adolescence—particularly among female participants, who reached an average HbA1c of 11.1% at ages 18–19—and that the proportion of overweight individuals (BMI >25.0 kg/m2) increased significantly from 21% to 54% in females and 2% to 28% in males. Furthermore, serious microvascular complications or hypertension developed in 25% of male and 38% of female participants, strongly correlating with higher mean HbA1c levels, while adolescent behavioral problems were identified as significant predictors of poorer subsequent glycemic control over the 8-year study period.
