This study title “Examining cognitive-behavioral therapy change mechanisms for decreasing depression, weight, and insulin resistance in adolescent girls at risk for type 2 diabetes” evaluates the cognitive-behavioral therapy change mechanisms for decreasing depression symptoms, body mass index, percent body fat, and insulin resistance in adolescent girls at risk for type 2 diabetes by comparing a six-week group cognitive-behavioral therapy intervention against a health education control program. The findings demonstrate that within the cognitive-behavioral therapy group, increased physical activity frequency and the perceived pleasantness of physical and social activities were indirectly associated with significant reductions in one-year body mass index via early decreases in depression symptoms, whereas these indirect pathways were not observed in the health education control condition. Based on these observed mechanisms, the authors recommend incorporating behavioral activation—specifically strategies targeting the frequency and enjoyment of physical and social activities—into targeted interventions to effectively alleviate depressive symptoms and prevent excess weight gain in adolescent girls at high risk for type 2 diabetes.
