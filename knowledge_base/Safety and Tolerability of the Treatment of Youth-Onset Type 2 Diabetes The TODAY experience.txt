The “Safety and Tolerability of the Treatment of Youth-Onset Type 2 Diabetes: The TODAY experience” evaluates the safety, tolerability, and event rates of three distinct treatment strategies—metformin monotherapy, metformin plus rosiglitazone, and metformin plus an intensive lifestyle intervention—in 699 obese adolescents aged 10–17 with youth-onset type 2 diabetes followed over 2 to 6.5 years. The study found that all three treatment approaches were generally safe and well tolerated, with low rates of treatment-emergent serious adverse events (SAEs) and no reported deaths. Gastrointestinal disturbance was the most common nonsevere adverse event (41%), though it occurred less frequently in the metformin plus rosiglitazone group, which also experienced significantly lower rates of study medication dose reductions or discontinuations due to elevated liver transaminases. Targeted serious adverse events were rare across all groups and included 12 cases of diabetic ketoacidosis, 5 of severe hypoglycemia, and 1 of lactic acidosis associated with severe asthma, alongside 62 pregnancies yielding 6 congenital anomalies. Based on these findings, the paper concludes that combining rosiglitazone with metformin may reduce gastrointestinal side effects and hepatotoxicity in youth with type 2 diabetes.
