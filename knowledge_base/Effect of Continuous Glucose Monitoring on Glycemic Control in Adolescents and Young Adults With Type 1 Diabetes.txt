Effect of Continuous Glucose Monitoring on Glycemic Control in Adolescents and Young Adults With Type 1 Diabetes: A Randomized Clinical Trial evaluates the impact of continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) compared with standard blood glucose monitoring (BGM) on glycemic control over 26 weeks in 153 adolescents and young adults aged 14 to 24 years with type 1 diabetes. The study found that treatment with CGM resulted in a statistically significant reduction in mean HbA1c levels from 8.9% at baseline to 8.5% at 26 weeks, compared to remaining unchanged at 8.9% in the BGM group (an adjusted between-group difference of -0.37%). Additionally, participants in the CGM group demonstrated improvements in secondary outcomes, including time spent in the target glucose range and reduced daytime and nighttime glucose variability. The study concludes that CGM leads to small but statistically significant improvements in glycemic control in this population and notes that further research is needed to determine the clinical importance and long-term implications of these findings.
