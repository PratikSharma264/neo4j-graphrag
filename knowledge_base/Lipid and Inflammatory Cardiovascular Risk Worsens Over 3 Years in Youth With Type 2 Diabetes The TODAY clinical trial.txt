"Lipid and Inflammatory Cardiovascular Risk Worsens Over 3 Years in Youth With Type 2 Diabetes: The TODAY clinical trial" evaluates lipid profiles (including LDL, HDL, triglycerides, non-HDL cholesterol, apolipoprotein B, and LDL particle size) and inflammatory risk markers (hsCRP, homocysteine, PAI-1, and nonesterified fatty acids) over 36 months in 699 multiethnic youth with recent-onset type 2 diabetes treated with metformin alone, metformin plus rosiglitazone, or metformin plus an intensive lifestyle program. The findings demonstrate that cardiovascular risk factors and dyslipidemia worsened significantly over time across all treatment groups, with overall increases in LDL, triglycerides, apolipoprotein B, non-HDL, hsCRP, homocysteine, and PAI-1, alongside a rise in participants meeting high-risk LDL thresholds from 4.5% to 10.7%. While intensive lifestyle intervention yielded lower triglyceride levels and rosiglitazone co-treatment reduced hsCRP compared to metformin alone, standard glucose-lowering treatments and protocol-driven statin adjustments were generally inadequate to arrest this worsening risk, leading to the paper's main recommendation and guideline implication that standard diabetes treatments are insufficient on their own and that aggressive, dedicated management of dyslipidemia and inflammatory risk factors is critical for youth with type 2 diabetes.
