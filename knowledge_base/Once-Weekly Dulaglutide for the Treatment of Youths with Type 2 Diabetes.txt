The "Once-Weekly Dulaglutide for the Treatment of Youths with Type 2 Diabetes" study evaluates the efficacy and safety of once-weekly subcutaneous dulaglutide injections (0.75 mg or 1.5 mg) compared to placebo over a 26-week double-blind period, followed by a 26-week open-label extension, in youth aged 10 to under 18 years with type 2 diabetes who were treated with lifestyle modifications alone or alongside metformin with or without basal insulin. The trial found that dulaglutide was superior to placebo in improving glycemic control, resulting in significant reductions in glycated hemoglobin levels (-0.6 percentage points in the 0.75-mg group and -0.9 percentage points in the 1.5-mg group compared to an increase of 0.6 percentage points in placebo) and fasting glucose concentrations, with a higher proportion of dulaglutide-treated participants achieving glycated hemoglobin levels below 7.0% (51% vs. 14%). However, treatment with dulaglutide showed no significant effect on body-mass index compared to placebo and was associated with a higher incidence of gastrointestinal adverse events, though its safety profile remained consistent with that previously reported in adults.
