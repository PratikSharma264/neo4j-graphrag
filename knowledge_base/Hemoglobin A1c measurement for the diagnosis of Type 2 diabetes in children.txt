“Hemoglobin A1c measurement for the diagnosis of Type 2 diabetes in children “ evaluates the validity, clinical utility, and methodological criticisms regarding the use of Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) for diagnosing Type 2 diabetes in pediatric populations compared to traditional fasting plasma glucose (FPG) and oral glucose tolerance testing (OGTT). The authors find that dismissing HbA1c for low sensitivity or specificity when compared to OGTT is scientifically unsound because neither OGTT nor FPG have been validated in children against long-term health outcomes like diabetic retinopathy, whereas HbA1c offers key advantages such as lower variability, better reproducibility, and not requiring a fasting state, despite potential limitations in cases of rapid-onset Type 1 diabetes or conditions affecting red blood cell turnover. Based on these findings, the guidelines and recommendations advise using American Diabetes Association diagnostic criteria—including an HbA1c threshold of 6.5% or higher—for screening asymptomatic at-risk children using a laboratory assay certified by the National Glycohemoglobin Standardization Program, while urging practitioners to use clinical judgment when results are discrepant or in patients with cystic fibrosis or hemoglobinopathies.
