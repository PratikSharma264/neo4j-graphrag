The “Lipid Screening and Cardiovascular Health in Childhood” evaluates the clinical guidelines and research surrounding childhood lipid screening, dyslipidemia, and cardiovascular health, replacing the previous 1998 policy statement. Synthesizing evidence from landmark studies like Bogalusa and PDAY, the report finds that atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease begins in childhood, tracks into adulthood, and is exacerbated by early risk factors like high LDL, low HDL, obesity, and the metabolic syndrome. To address these risks, the paper recommends a targeted screening strategy using fasting lipid profiles for children and adolescents with a positive family history of premature cardiovascular disease or high cholesterol, as well as for those with unknown family histories or co-occurring risk factors like obesity, hypertension, and diabetes. The guidelines emphasize cardiovascular disease prevention through lifestyle modifications—specifically adhering to the Dietary Guidelines for Americans and increasing daily physical activity—while introducing updated recommendations for pharmacologic treatment, such as statin therapy, for children with severe dyslipidemia who fail lifestyle intervention.
