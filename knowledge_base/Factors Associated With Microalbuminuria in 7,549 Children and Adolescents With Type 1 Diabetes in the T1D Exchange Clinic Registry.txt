The study “Factors Associated With Microalbuminuria in 7,549 Children and Adolescents With Type 1 Diabetes in the T1D Exchange Clinic Registry “ evaluates the frequency of microalbuminuria (MA) clinical diagnosis and its associated risk factors among children and adolescents under 20 years of age with type 1 diabetes across 67 U.S. pediatric and adult endocrinology practices. Analyzing a cohort of 7,549 participants, the study found an overall MA prevalence of 4.4% (329 participants), with a higher frequency strongly associated with longer diabetes duration, higher mean glycosylated hemoglobin HbA1c, older age, female sex, higher diastolic blood pressure, and lower BMI z score, while MA was uncommon (<2%) in individuals with an average HbA1c <7.5%. Despite MA diagnosis, only 36% of affected participants were currently receiving ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker treatment. Based on these findings, the authors emphasize the critical need for optimal glycemic and blood pressure control to reduce nephropathy risk, and they highlight ADA screening guidelines recommending annual MA screening in individuals with type 1 diabetes starting at 10 years of age and 5 years of diabetes duration to ensure early diagnosis and timely intervention.
