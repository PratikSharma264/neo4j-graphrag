"Health Care Transition Preparation and Experiences in a U.S. National Sample of Young Adults With Type 1 Diabetes" evaluates the transition experiences, preparation, and glycemic outcomes of 602 young adults with type 1 diabetes across two groups: those still receiving pediatric care and those who had already transitioned to adult care. The study found that while pediatric providers frequently counseled patients on self-management and screening tests, important topics like reproductive health and alcohol use were less commonly discussed, and emotional attachment to pediatric providers often delayed transitions. Among transitioned participants, 21% experienced a gap in care of more than six months, which was significantly associated with having fewer pre-transition pediatric visits or feeling unprepared for the transition. Although receipt of transition preparation counseling was not directly associated with achieving target hemoglobin A1c levels, overall feelings of preparedness were linked to lower diabetes-related distress and better self-care scores. Based on these findings, the paper recommends intensive, integrated efforts to incorporate structured transition preparation counseling and care coordination into pediatric diabetes care to minimize gaps and better support young adults.
