"Prevalence and Correlates of Depressed Mood Among Youth With Diabetes: The SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth Study" evaluates whether depressed mood among youth with diabetes is associated with diabetes type, duration, mean glycosylated hemoglobin HbA1c level, and the frequency of diabetic ketoacidosis, hypoglycemic episodes, hospitalizations, and emergency department visits. In a sample of 2,672 youth aged 10 to 21 years with a mean diabetes duration of 5 years, 14% exhibited mildly depressed mood (CES-D 16–23) and 8.6% exhibited moderately or severely depressed mood (CES-D ≥ 24), with overall prevalence rates comparable to published estimates for youth without diabetes. Females reported higher mean depression scores than males, and after adjusting for demographic factors and diabetes duration, depressed mood was found to be higher among males with type 2 diabetes than those with type 1 diabetes, as well as higher among females with comorbidities than those without. Higher mean HbA1c  levels and higher frequency of emergency department visits were significantly associated with depressed mood, prompting the recommendation that physicians and healthcare professionals consider screening youth with diabetes for depressed mood in clinical settings, especially targeting youth with poor glycemic control, frequent emergency department visits, males with type 2 diabetes, and females with comorbidities.
