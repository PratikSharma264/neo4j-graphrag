The Projections of Type 1 and Type 2 Diabetes Burden in the U.S. Population Aged <20 Years Through 2060: The SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth Stud evaluates projections of type 1 and type 2 diabetes prevalence and case counts among U.S. youth aged under 20 through the year 2060, analyzing both constant incidence trends and continuing historical incidence growth patterns. The study finds that if 2017 incidence levels remain constant, youth diabetes cases will increase from 213,000 in 2017 (185,000 type 1; 28,000 type 2) to 239,000 in 2060 (191,000 type 1; 48,000 type 2), reflecting a 3% relative rise for type 1 and a 69% relative rise for type 2 diabetes. However, if the increasing incidence trends observed between 2002 and 2017 persist, total youth diabetes cases are projected to reach 526,000 by 2060 (306,000 type 1; 220,000 type 2)—representing a 65% relative increase for type 1 and a 673% relative increase for type 2 diabetes—with substantial widening of racial and ethnic disparities leading to the highest prevalence among non-Hispanic Black youth. Based on these projections, the paper highlights the urgent need for targeted prevention strategies to attenuate the rising burden of youth diabetes in future decades.
