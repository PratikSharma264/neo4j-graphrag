Assessing fear of hypoglycemia in children with Type 1 diabetes and their parents evaluates the literature and measurement instruments surrounding fear of hypoglycemia (FoH) in youth with Type 1 diabetes and their parents, focusing primarily on the pediatric and parental adaptations of the Hypoglycemia Fear Survey (HFS). Findings indicate that FoH is highly prevalent among children and caregivers, driven by factors such as past severe or nocturnal hypoglycemic events, supervision concerns when away from home, and general trait anxiety, with mothers consistently reporting higher fear levels than fathers or adult patients. Additionally, the paper demonstrates that younger children (ages 6–18) can provide reliable self-reports of FoH, though evidence linking high FoH to altered glycemic control (HbA1c) remains inconsistent across clinical studies. Based on these insights, the paper emphasizes recommendations for clinicians to routinely evaluate FoH in pediatric patients and parents to detect maladaptive behaviors—such as maintaining deliberately elevated blood glucose levels to avoid drops—and highlights guidelines calling for expanded research across broader age groups to refine developmental assessment and intervention strategies.
