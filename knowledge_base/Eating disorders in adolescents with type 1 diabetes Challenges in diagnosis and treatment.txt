"Eating disorders in adolescents with type 1 diabetes: Challenges in diagnosis and treatment" evaluates the predisposing factors, clinical presentation, complications, and management of eating disorders (ED) and disordered eating behaviors—specifically intentional insulin omission—among adolescents and young adults with type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM). The review finds that insulin omission for weight loss affects up to 40% of young adult females with T1DM, driven by a combination of premorbid factors (such as tendency toward higher body weight, low self-esteem, and family dynamics around food), factors at disease onset (like age at diagnosis and initial weight changes), and chronic diabetes management stressors (including intensive insulin-induced weight gain, rigid dietary restrictions, recurrent hypoglycemia, and depression). These behaviors lead to significantly poorer glycemic control (frequently marked by elevated HbA1c  levels exceeding 9.2% and extreme fluctuations), recurrent diabetic ketoacidosis, severe hypoglycemia, and substantially higher long-term risks for microvascular complications such as retinopathy, nephropathy, and foot problems. To address this hidden burden, the paper recommends maintaining a high index of suspicion and utilizing diabetes-specific screening tools like the revised Diabetes Eating Problem Survey (DEPS-R) or mSCOFF, alongside tracking clinical prediction patterns in HbA1c  trajectories. The guidelines highlight that effective management requires early recognition and a collaborative, multidisciplinary team approach combining medical, dietary, and psychosocial interventions tailored to the complex interplay of diabetes management and psychological health.
