"Clinic attendance and disengagement of young adults with type 1 diabetes after transition of care from pediatric to adult services (TrACeD): a randomised, open-label, controlled trial" evaluates the effect of an appointment-management intervention—which involved personalized pre-appointment telephone and SMS reminders along with automatic rebooking of missed appointments—on clinic attendance and service disengagement among 120 young adults aged 17–19 years transitioning from pediatric to adult diabetes services in Melbourne, Australia. The study's findings indicate that during the initial 0–12 months post-transition, the appointment-management intervention did not significantly increase clinic attendance or decrease service disengagement compared to current care. However, at 12–24 months post-transition, the intervention demonstrated a significant positive effect, resulting in higher mean clinic attendance (2.5 vs. 1.4 visits) and a substantially lower rate of disengagement from services (6% vs. 49%) compared to the control group. Neither the intervention nor pre-transition clinic attendance had an independent effect on glycated hemoglobin concentrations after transition.
