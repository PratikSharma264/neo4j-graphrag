"Rethinking funding priorities in mental health research" evaluates the distribution of research investments in high-income countries—particularly at the US National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH)—arguing that a heavy skew toward long-term, neurobiological mechanism research comes at the expense of shorter-term prevention and treatment strategies. The paper finds that basic and translational neuroscience accounts for approximately 85% of the NIMH non-HIV/AIDS budget, leaving only around 15% for services and interventions research, despite substantial immediate public health needs and high rates of unmet mental health care. The authors recommend a rebalancing and diversification of the mental health research portfolio to allocate greater funding toward scalable, cost-efficient interventions, implementation science, suicide prevention, and addressing social determinants of health. Finally, they provide guidelines calling for broad, international stakeholder participation across public, legislative, and scientific forums to re-evaluate funding allocations, evaluate implementation evidence in routine practice settings, and ensure immediate public health impacts alongside the long-term search for neurobiological cures.
