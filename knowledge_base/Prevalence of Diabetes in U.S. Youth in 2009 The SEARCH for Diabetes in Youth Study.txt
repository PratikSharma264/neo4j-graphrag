“Prevalence of Diabetes in U.S. Youth in 2009: The SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth Study” evaluates the prevalence of physician-diagnosed diabetes among youth aged under 20 years in the United States in 2009 by age, sex, race/ethnicity, and diabetes type. The study found that an estimated 191,986 U.S. youth had diabetes (a crude prevalence of 2.22 per 1,000), consisting of 166,984 with type 1 diabetes (1.93 per 1,000), 20,262 with type 2 diabetes (0.24 per 1,000), and 4,740 with other types. Prevalence increased with age, was slightly higher in females, and varied significantly across racial/ethnic groups: non-Hispanic White youth had the highest prevalence of type 1 diabetes, while minority groups—particularly Native American and non-Hispanic Black youth—exhibited the highest prevalence of type 2 diabetes. The paper did not explicitly outline specific clinical recommendations or guidelines.
