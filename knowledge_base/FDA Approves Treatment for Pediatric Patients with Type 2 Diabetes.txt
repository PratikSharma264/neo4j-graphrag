"FDA Approves Treatment for Pediatric Patients with Type 2 Diabetes" evaluates the efficacy and safety of exenatide extended-release injection (Bydureon and Bydureon BCise) as an adjunct to diet and exercise for improving glycemic control in pediatric patients aged 10 years or older with type 2 diabetes. In a 24-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 82 pediatric patients, treatment with 2 mg once weekly resulted in an average 0.25% decrease in HbA1c compared to an average 0.45% increase in the placebo group, demonstrating a significant treatment difference of -0.70%. The drug carries a boxed warning regarding the risk of thyroid C-cell tumors and is contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2, prior serious allergic reactions to exenatide, or drug-induced immune-mediated thrombocytopenia. Additional warnings highlight risks of pancreatitis, hypoglycemia when combined with insulin secretagogues or insulin, acute kidney injury, severe gastrointestinal disease, acute gallbladder disease, and serious allergic reactions, with overall side effects—such as injection site reactions, headaches, and gastrointestinal issues—mirroring those seen in adult populations. Guidelines specify that exenatide extended-release is not recommended as a first-line therapy for patients inadequately controlled by diet and exercise alone, should not be used to treat type 1 diabetes, and must not be used concurrently with other exenatide-containing products.
