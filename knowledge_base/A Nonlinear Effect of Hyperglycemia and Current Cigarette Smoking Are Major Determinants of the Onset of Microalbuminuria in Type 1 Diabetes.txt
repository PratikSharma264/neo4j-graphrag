"A Nonlinear Effect of Hyperglycemia and Current Cigarette Smoking Are Major Determinants of the Onset of Microalbuminuria in Type 1 Diabetes" evaluates the temporal and joint effects of glycemic control (measured by HbA1c) and cigarette smoking status on the 4-year incidence of microalbuminuria among 943 young adults with type 1 diabetes from the Natural History of Microalbuminuria Study. The findings demonstrate that microalbuminuria developed at a rate of 3.3 per 100 person-years, with recent HbA1c levels (from the 1st and 2nd years preceding onset) serving as a stronger predictor of risk than older or cumulative 4-year measurements, indicating an attenuation of the impact of past hyperglycemia over time. The study identified a significant nonlinear relationship between HbA1c and microalbuminuria risk, showing a markedly steeper increase in risk once HbA1c exceeds 8%, a risk enhancement that is further magnified among current cigarette smokers (who experienced an incidence rate of 7.9 per 100 person-years compared to 1.8 for past smokers and 2.2 for never-smokers). While no explicit clinical guidelines or recommendations are formally established in the text, the authors conclude that patients with type 1 diabetes who smoke and maintain HbA1c levels above 8% face the highest risk for developing microalbuminuria.
