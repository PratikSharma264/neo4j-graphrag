Diabetes Care in the School Setting: A Statement of the American Diabetes Association evaluates the current guidelines, legal framework, and best practices for managing diabetes in children and adolescents across pre-K through 12th-grade school environments. The document reports a significant rise in diabetes among youth, noting a relative increase of forty-five percent in type 1 diabetes prevalence over the past two decades and a near doubling of type 2 diabetes cases over the same period, particularly among racial and ethnic minoritized populations. It highlights that diabetes is legally protected under federal statutes such as Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act, the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act, and the Americans with Disabilities Act, prohibiting schools that receive federal funds from discriminating against affected students or refusing accommodations due to limited nursing resources. Furthermore, the paper emphasizes that continuous glucose monitors, automated insulin delivery systems, and real-time remote monitoring significantly improve glycemic control and lower the burden of care, though schools must appropriately adapt to accommodate these technological tools without creating unnecessary classroom disruptions. To ensure safety and equal access to education, the paper recommends establishing individualized care plans—such as a 504 Plan or Individualized Education Program—that are directly grounded in a medical provider's Diabetes Medical Management Plan. It explicitly states that schools cannot require parents to attend field trips or assist with routine care to enable their child's participation. The statement outlines guidelines requiring schools to train non-nurse personnel to assist with routine care, monitor glucose levels, and administer both insulin and glucagon during emergencies when a nurse is unavailable. Additionally, it directs school staff to provide advance carbohydrate counts for cafeteria meals, ensure fast-acting carbohydrates are readily accessible during physical activities, support necessary device connectivity like Wi-Fi and phone access, and address the behavioral and mental health needs associated with managing chronic disease in a school environment.
