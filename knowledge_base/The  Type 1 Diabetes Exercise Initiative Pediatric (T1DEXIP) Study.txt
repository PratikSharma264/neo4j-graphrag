The  Type 1 Diabetes Exercise Initiative Pediatric (T1DEXIP) Study evaluates 24-hour postexercise glycemia and overnight hypoglycemia risk in adolescents with type 1 diabetes in a real-world setting, analyzing data from 251 participants across 3,319 exercise sessions. The study found that maximal glucose drops occurred 8 to 16 hours after exercise, with larger drops during activity being associated with lower mean glucose levels immediately post-exercise and 12 to 16 hours later; overall, nocturnal hypoglycemia occurred on 14% of post-exercise nights (compared to 12% on sedentary days), with significantly higher rates when average total daily activity was at least 60 minutes, during longer individual exercise sessions, in youth with longer diabetes duration or lower HbA1c, with conventional pump use, and when time below range was at least 4% in the preceding 24 hours. While explicit clinical guidelines were not provided in the paper, the authors highlight that regular physical activity should be prescribed and encouraged for youth with type 1 diabetes (matching the general pediatric recommendation of 60 minutes daily) and recommend leveraging these participant- and event-level findings to inform new clinical guidelines, decision support tools, and refined automated closed-loop insulin delivery algorithms to better mitigate exercise-related hypoglycemia.
