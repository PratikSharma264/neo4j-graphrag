The Type 1 Diabetes Exercise Initiative Pediatric (T1DEXIP) study evaluates the participant- and event-level factors that influence 24-hour postexercise glycemia and overnight hypoglycemia risk in free-living youth with type 1 diabetes. Analyzing 3,319 exercise sessions across 251 adolescents, the study found that maximal glucose drops occurred 8 to 16 hours after exercise, with larger exercise-induced glucose drops predicting lower postexercise mean glucose levels. Postexercise overnight hypoglycemia occurred on 14% of nights overall and was significantly higher among participants with higher average daily activity (> 60 minutes per day), longer individual exercise sessions ( >90 minutes), longer diabetes duration, lower HbA1c, conventional pump use, and elevated time below range (>=4%) in the prior 24 hours. Based on these findings, the paper indicates that characterization of these exercise recovery patterns can inform updated clinical guidelines, enhance decision-support tools, and refine closed-loop automated insulin delivery algorithms to better manage physical activity in pediatric type 1 diabetes.
