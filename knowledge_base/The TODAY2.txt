The TODAY2 study evaluates the prevalence and associated factors of high diabetes distress among young adults with youth-onset type 2 diabetes. Analyzing 438 participants, the researchers found that 24% reported high overall diabetes distress, with 40% experiencing high regimen distress and 29.7% experiencing high emotional burden. High distress was significantly associated with female sex, higher HbA1c levels, moderate-to-severe anxiety symptoms, and a lack of healthcare coverage, while moderate-to-severe depressive symptoms were linked to both regimen distress and emotional burden, and insulin treatment was specifically linked to emotional burden. The paper recommends that future research focus on identifying modifiable factors associated with high diabetes distress in order to inform targeted psychosocial interventions for this medically vulnerable group.
