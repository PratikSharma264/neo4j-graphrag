Early Initiation of Diabetes Devices Relates to Improved Glycemic Control in Children with Recent-Onset Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus evaluates whether initiating an insulin pump or a continuous glucose monitor (CGM) within the first year of diagnosis is associated with reduced glycated hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) levels in a cohort of 111 children aged 5 to 9 years with recent-onset type 1 diabetes. Tracking participants from baseline (mean diabetes duration of 4.7 months) to a 6-month follow-up (~12 months post-diagnosis), the study found that insulin pump usage grew from 17% to 35.1% and CGM usage increased from 17.1% to 25.2%. The findings demonstrate that children using an insulin pump maintained lower overall HbA1c levels compared to non-users, and children who started using a CGM during this timeframe experienced significantly lower HbA1c levels than those who did not. Based on these findings, the authors conclude and recommend that early initiation of either an insulin pump or a CGM in newly diagnosed children can help improve glycemic control within their first year of living with type 1 diabetes.
