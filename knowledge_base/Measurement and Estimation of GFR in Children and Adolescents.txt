“Measurement and Estimation of GFR in Children and Adolescents” evaluates various methods and markers for determining glomerular filtration rate (GFR) to assess renal function in pediatric populations. The authors find that while classic inulin clearance remains the gold standard, its clinical utility is severely limited by assay complexity, poor availability, and the challenge of timed urine collections in young or functionally impaired children. Radioisotopes like (_^99"m" )"Tc-DTPA"  and (_^125)"I-iothalamate"  present measurement challenges or undergo tubular secretion, whereas creatinine-based estimates can falsely elevate GFR unless cimetidine is used to block tubular secretion. Ultimately, the paper recommends iohexol plasma disappearance as the optimal alternative, citing its exceptional safety profile, ease of measurement, lack of renal metabolism or tubular transport, and high correlation with multi-compartment disappearance models. Based on these findings, the study highlights guidelines for optimizing sampling protocols—such as using a streamlined four-point or five-point blood sampling schedule over at least 5 hours (or up to 24 hours in severe chronic kidney disease)—and suggests fingerprick blood spot sampling as a practical, scalable approach for outpatient and epidemiological GFR monitoring.
