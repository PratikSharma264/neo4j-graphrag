The systematic review Screening and identification of disordered eating in people with type 1 diabetes: A systematic review evaluates the reliability, validity, and clinical utility of 48 individual tools used to screen or identify disordered eating behaviors and eating disorders in individuals with Type 1 Diabetes across 90 studies. The authors found that the overall quality of the reported tools was poor, with a high risk of bias stemming from the widespread use of non-validated assessment tools and a lack of comparison against the reference standard of a diagnostic clinical interview. While the review notes that current guidelines suggest routine screening for disordered eating in people with Type 1 Diabetes due to elevated risk and associated health complications, it highlights a critical lack of evidence regarding which specific tools are most valid and reliable across different age groups and clinical settings. Consequently, the authors recommend that future research focus on evaluating the validity and reliability of available screening tools by directly comparing them against gold-standard diagnostic interviews to help establish standardized screening guidelines and improve clinical outcomes.
