"Neuropsychiatric disorders at the presentation of type 2 diabetes mellitus in children" evaluates the frequency of neuropsychiatric disease (NPD) and psychotropic medication use in an urban pediatric population with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) by conducting a retrospective chart review of 237 T2DM patients at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia. The study found that 46 patients (19.4%) had an NPD diagnosis—including depression, ADHD, neurodevelopmental disorders, schizophrenia, and bipolar disorder—at the time of T2DM presentation, with no statistically significant differences in demographic characteristics or BMI between affected and unaffected groups, and that 63% of affected patients were taking psychotropic medications (most commonly mood stabilizers and atypical antipsychotics). Based on these findings, the paper suggests implications for establishing screening regimens for pediatric populations at high risk for T2DM and for guiding therapeutic interventions, including lifestyle measures.
