The SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth Study Group evaluated changes in the prevalence of physician-diagnosed type 1 and type 2 diabetes among children and adolescents under age 20 across six US regions between 2001, 2009, and 2017. Utilizing a cross-sectional observational design, researchers assessed a mean population of 3.47 million youths per prevalence year to determine shifts in youth-onset diabetes rates over time across various demographic groups. Over the 16-year period, estimated prevalence increased significantly for both conditions. Type 1 diabetes prevalence among youths aged 19 and under rose from 1.48 per 1,000 in 2001 to 2.15 per 1,000 in 2017, representing a 45.1% relative increase, with the largest absolute gains occurring among non-Hispanic White and non-Hispanic Black youths. Type 2 diabetes prevalence among adolescents aged 10 to 19 nearly doubled, rising from 0.34 per 1,000 in 2001 to 0.67 per 1,000 in 2017—a 95.3% relative increase—with non-Hispanic Black and Hispanic youths experiencing the greatest absolute increases. While the primary study was strictly epidemiological and did not issue formal clinical practice guidelines or actionable policy recommendations, it highlights the critical need to anticipate and allocate clinical resources to manage the growing burden of youth-onset diabetes. Furthermore, the study notes that understanding these demographic trends is essential for targeting clinical interventions and public health efforts toward the specific racial and ethnic groups facing the steepest rises in disease burden.
