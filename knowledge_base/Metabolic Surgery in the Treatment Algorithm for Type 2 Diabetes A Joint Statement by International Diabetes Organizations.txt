"Metabolic Surgery in the Treatment Algorithm for Type 2 Diabetes: A Joint Statement by International Diabetes Organizations" evaluates the role, efficacy, and clinical indications of bariatric/metabolic surgery as an intervention for managing type 2 diabetes (T2D) alongside traditional medical and lifestyle therapies. The study findings demonstrate that gastrointestinal metabolic surgery achieves superior, durable glycemic control and significant reduction of cardiovascular risk factors compared to standard medical interventions. Based on these insights, the guidelines recommend metabolic surgery as a standard treatment option for T2D in candidates with class III obesity BMI≥40 kg/m² regardless of glycemic control, as well as for patients with class II obesity BMI  35.0-39.9 kg/m² when hyperglycemia is inadequately controlled by optimal medical therapy. Additionally, the guidelines state that surgery should be considered for patients with class I obesity BMI 30.0-34.9 kg/m² who experience inadequate control despite oral or injectable medications, noting that all specified BMI thresholds should be lowered by 2.5 kg/m² for patients of Asian descent. Finally, the recommendations emphasize performing operations in high-volume, multidisciplinary centers with long-term post-operative nutritional monitoring and urge healthcare regulators to update reimbursement policies to accommodate surgical diabetes care.
