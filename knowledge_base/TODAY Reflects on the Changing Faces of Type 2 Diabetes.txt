The "TODAY Reflects on the Changing Faces of Type 2 Diabetes" study evaluates the efficacy, safety, and longitudinal outcomes of three treatment strategies—metformin alone, metformin plus rosiglitazone, and metformin plus intensive lifestyle changes—in maintaining glycemic control among adolescents aged 10 to 17 years with recent-onset type 2 diabetes. The paper highlights findings demonstrating that metformin monotherapy achieved durable glycemic control in only about half of the youth, whereas the combination of metformin and rosiglitazone significantly improved glycemic durability due to favorable effects on insulin sensitivity and beta-cell function. Although the treatment approaches were generally safe with primarily gastrointestinal side effects, the study interventions were inadequate to prevent the progression of cardiovascular and renal risk factors, as dyslipidemia, chronic inflammation, hypertension, and microalbuminuria worsened over time regardless of diabetes treatment. Considering projections of up to a fourfold increase in youth type 2 diabetes, the commentary emphasizes the critical recommendation for immediate research, screening, medical interventions, and public policy changes to slow and reverse the growing health and economic burden of adolescent-onset type 2 diabetes.
