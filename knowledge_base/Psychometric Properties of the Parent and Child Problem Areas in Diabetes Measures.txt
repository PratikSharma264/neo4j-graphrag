Psychometric Properties of the Parent and Child Problem Areas in Diabetes Measures evaluates the validity and reliability of adapted child (PAID-C) and parent (P-PAID-C) versions of the Problem Areas in Diabetes Scale designed to assess diabetes-specific emotional distress in children ages 8–12 with type 1 diabetes and their caregivers across 42 summer camps in the United States. The study found that the 11-item, two-factor child measure (covering emotional burden and regimen-specific distress; Cronbach’s α = 0.91) and the 16-item parent measure (structured around four factors: negative emotions, keeping up with chronic demands, personal regimen-specific distress, and child regimen-specific distress; Cronbach’s α = 0.92) both demonstrated strong internal consistency and construct validity. Higher distress scores on both versions significantly correlated with higher HbA1c levels, lower diabetes-related strengths, and lower parent-reported self-care skills, supporting criterion validity. Based on these findings, the paper recommends incorporating routine, clinic-based screening using the PAID-C and P-PAID-C to identify diabetes distress early, facilitate targeted interventions, and guide clinical decision-making to prevent diabetes burnout and regimen mismanagement.
