"Weight Loss and Health Status 3 Years after Bariatric Surgery in Adolescents" evaluates the efficacy and safety of weight-loss surgery—specifically Roux-en-Y gastric bypass and sleeve gastrectomy—among 242 adolescents with severe obesity across five U.S. centers over a three-year postoperative follow-up period. The study finds significant long-term health improvements, including an average body weight reduction of 27% across the entire cohort (28% for gastric bypass and 26% for sleeve gastrectomy) and high remission rates for baseline coexisting conditions: 95% for type 2 diabetes, 86% for abnormal kidney function, 76% for prediabetes, 74% for elevated blood pressure, and 66% for dyslipidemia, alongside substantial improvements in weight-related quality of life. However, the findings also identify noteworthy postoperative risks, showing that 57% of participants developed hypoferritinemia (low ferritin levels) by year three, 13% required one or more additional intraabdominal procedures, and deficiencies in specific micronutrients such as vitamins B1 and A were observed. While the paper does not propose specific clinical guidelines or formal recommendations, its results highlight that the significant cardiometabolic benefits of bariatric surgery in adolescents must be weighed against risks requiring ongoing surgical and nutritional monitoring.
