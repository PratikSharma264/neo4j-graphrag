The study "Systematic Review: Diabetes Family Conflict in Young People With Type 1 Diabetes" evaluates the relationships between diabetes-specific family conflict and various health outcomes—including glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), treatment adherence, blood glucose monitoring, depression, anxiety, quality of life, and well-being—among young people with type 1 diabetes aged 14 to 25 years. Across 20 included studies, greater diabetes-specific family conflict was significantly associated with higher HbA1c values in 17 studies, suboptimal treatment adherence or less frequent blood glucose monitoring in 7 studies, and poorer quality of life, reduced well-being, or heightened depressive and anxiety symptoms in 7 studies, though 4 studies found no significant links to HbA1c or adherence. Given these findings, the authors recommend routine screening for and intervention addressing diabetes-specific family conflict in clinical practice, while highlighting guidelines for future research to prioritize longitudinal studies specifically targeting young people over the age of 16.
