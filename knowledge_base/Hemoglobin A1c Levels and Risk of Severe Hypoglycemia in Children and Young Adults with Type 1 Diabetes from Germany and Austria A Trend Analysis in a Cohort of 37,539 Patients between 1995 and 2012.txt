The study Hemoglobin A1c Levels and Risk of Severe Hypoglycemia in Children and Young Adults with Type 1 Diabetes from Germany and Austria: A Trend Analysis in a Cohort of 37,539 Patients between 1995 and 2012 evaluates temporal trends in the association between hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) levels and the risk of severe hypoglycemia or hypoglycemic coma in a prospective cohort of 37,539 children and young adults with type 1 diabetes from Germany and Austria between 1995 and 2012. The study found that the relative risk for severe hypoglycemia and coma per 1% HbA1c decrease declined significantly from 1.28 to 1.05 and from 1.39 to 1.01, respectively, over the 18-year period, alongside a continuous increase in daily blood glucose monitoring frequency, short- and long-acting insulin analog use, and continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion pump therapy. Based on these findings, the paper indicates that lower HbA1c levels have become a minor predictor for severe hypoglycemia, allowing clinicians and young patients with type 1 diabetes to aim for near-normal glycemic control to prevent long-term diabetic complications without substantially increasing their risk of severe hypoglycemic events.
