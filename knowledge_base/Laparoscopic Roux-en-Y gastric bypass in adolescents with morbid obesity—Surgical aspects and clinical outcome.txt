"Laparoscopic Roux-en-Y gastric bypass in adolescents with morbid obesity—Surgical aspects and clinical outcome" evaluates the two-year clinical outcomes and five-year surgical adverse event rates of laparoscopic Roux-en-Y gastric bypass in 81 morbidly obese adolescents compared to age-, sex-, and BMI-matched adolescents undergoing conventional lifestyle care as well as adult surgical controls. The study finds that adolescents receiving gastric bypass achieved a 32% total weight loss (76% excess weight loss) after two years—comparable to the 31% weight loss observed in adults, whereas conventionally treated adolescents gained 3% body weight—alongside marked improvements in cardiovascular risk factors, metabolic health, systemic inflammation, and quality of life. While no postoperative mortality occurred, long-term surgical adverse events included cholecystectomies in 10% and reoperations for internal hernias in 9% of cases. Based on these observations, the paper recommends strict inclusion guidelines for adolescent bariatric surgery—requiring candidates to be aged 13–18, have a BMI of at least 40 kg/m² (or 35 kg/m² with severe comorbidities), have completed over one year of comprehensive conventional weight management, be past Tanner stage III and peak height velocity, and commit to daily multivitamin, mineral, calcium, vitamin D, and iron supplementation alongside preventive surgical technical refinements such as closing mesenteric defect windows.
