The “Diabetes Screening With Hemoglobin A1c Versus Fasting Plasma Glucose in a Multiethnic Middle-School Cohort” evaluates the distribution, durability, and association of high-risk hemoglobin A1c (5.7–6.4%) compared with impaired fasting glucose (100–125 mg/dL) as glycemic risk markers for type 2 diabetes in a multiethnic cohort of 3,980 sixth-grade students followed through eighth grade. The study found that impaired fasting glucose was roughly five times more common at baseline than high-risk A_1c (16.0% vs. 3.2%), but high-risk A1c was more persistent over a two-year period (59.4% persistence vs. 46.9% for impaired fasting glucose). Additionally, high-risk A_1c was significantly associated with non-Hispanic Black race/ethnicity, family history of diabetes, higher BMI, waist circumference, and fasting insulin, whereas impaired fasting glucose was associated with male sex, Hispanic ethnicity, obesity measures, fasting insulin, high blood pressure, and higher triglycerides, indicating that the two tests identify distinct groups of youth with differing diabetes and cardiovascular risk profiles. Ultimately, the authors conclude that optimal screening strategies for type 2 diabetes in youth remain unresolved due to these distinct clinical profiles and persistence patterns.
