"The effects of antipsychotics on weight gain, weight-related hormones and homocysteine in children and adolescents: a 1-year follow-up study" evaluates the longitudinal impact of second-generation antipsychotic medications—specifically risperidone, olanzapine, and quetiapine—on body weight, metabolic hormones, and homocysteine levels over a 12-month period in 117 antipsychotic-naïve or quasi-naïve youth. The study found that patients experienced significant overall increases in weight, BMI, and BMI z-scores across 3, 6, and 12 months, with quetiapine associated with the highest weight increases by year one; additionally, leptin and thyroid-stimulating hormone levels rose, free thyroxine levels decreased, and baseline factors such as higher insulin resistance, elevated leptin, or pre-existing overweight/obesity predicted greater weight gain. Based on these findings, the paper highlights that awareness and ongoing monitoring of weight-related and metabolic parameters in young patients are essential to help guide clinical decisions regarding antipsychotic prescriptions.
