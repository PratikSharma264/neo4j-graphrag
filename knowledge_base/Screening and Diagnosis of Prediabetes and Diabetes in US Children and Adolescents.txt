The study titled "Screening and Diagnosis of Prediabetes and Diabetes in US Children and Adolescents" evaluates the performance of American Diabetes Association risk-based screening criteria and clinical definitions of prediabetes and diabetes—based on hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) and fasting plasma glucose (FPG)—to identify US youth aged 10 to 19 years at high cardiometabolic risk using 1999–2016 NHANES data. The study found that while roughly 25.5% (10.6 million) of US youth were eligible for screening under 2018 ADA criteria, confirmed undiagnosed diabetes was extremely rare (<0.5%), with over 85% of total diabetes cases already diagnosed. Furthermore, the risk-based screening criteria exhibited low sensitivity and specificity for identifying hyperglycemia, though HbA1c-defined hyperglycemia showed significantly stronger and more specific associations with cardiometabolic risk factors (like obesity and metabolic syndrome) than FPG-defined hyperglycemia. Based on these findings, the authors recommend using HbA1c as a practical, specific nonfasting test to identify high-risk youth who could benefit from lifestyle interventions to prevent diabetes and cardiovascular risk in adulthood, highlighting American Diabetes Association guidelines that suggest screening asymptomatic children aged 10 and older (or after pubertal onset) who are overweight or obese and possess at least one additional risk factor.
