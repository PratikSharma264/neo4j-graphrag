Multisite Examination of Depression Screening Scores and Correlates Among Adolescents and Young Adults With Type 2 Diabetes evaluates self-reported depressive symptoms and clinical outcomes during routine screening for adolescents and young adults with type 2 diabetes (T2D) across four academic medical centers, as well as the associations between these depressive symptoms and various demographic and clinical characteristics. The study found that 19.3% of participants endorsed elevated depressive symptoms, 18.9% of a subgroup with item-level data endorsed thoughts of self-harm, and higher depressive symptoms were significantly associated with older age, shorter diabetes duration, higher A1C levels, being non-Hispanic White, more frequent daily blood glucose checks, and oral medication use. Additionally, only 50% of those screening positive for elevated depressive symptoms had a documented mental health referral in their electronic health record. The authors highlight the need for improved processes regarding mental health service referrals, standardized documentation of symptoms and referrals in electronic health records, dedicated self-harm assessments, and expanded mental health resources for this population.
