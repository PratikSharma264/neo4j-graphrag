The Hvidoere Childhood Diabetes Study Group Centre Differences Study 2005 evaluates the glycaemic targets set by diabetes teams, how these targets are perceived by adolescents with type 1 diabetes and their parents, and their overall influence on metabolic control across 21 international centres. In an assessment of 2,062 adolescents, the study found a mean HbA1c of 8.2%, with significant variation between centres ranging from 7.4% to 9.1%. Key findings revealed a significant positive correlation between the HbA1c levels that adolescents and parents reported they would be happy with—as well as their perceived ideal targets—and their actual HbA1c results, with lower target HbA1c setting and greater consistency among multidisciplinary team members directly associating with lower overall centre HbA1c levels. Ultimately, the paper concludes that target setting plays a significant role in driving the metabolic differences observed across centres, demonstrating that clear and consistent setting of glycaemic targets by pediatric diabetes teams strongly improves HbA1c outcomes.
