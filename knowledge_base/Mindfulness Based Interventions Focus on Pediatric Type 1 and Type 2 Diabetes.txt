Mindfulness-Based Interventions: Focus on Pediatric Type 1 and Type 2 Diabetes evaluates the literature surrounding the potential psychosocial and physical health benefits of mindfulness-based interventions (MBIs)—such as Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) and Learning to BREATHE—for youth diagnosed with or at risk for type 1 and type 2 diabetes. Findings indicate that while MBIs consistently reduce depressive symptoms and diabetes-specific distress in adolescents, their effects on objective health indicators like glycemic control (HbA1c) remain mixed and inconclusive across studies. To address these gaps, the authors highlight guidelines and recommendations for future research, including the systematic development of tailored, disease-specific MBI curricula (such as the BREATHE-T1D adaptation), the utilization of virtual delivery methods like telehealth to improve youth engagement, and the execution of larger-scale longitudinal trials to clarify underlying physiological mechanisms and facilitate integration into standard clinical care.
