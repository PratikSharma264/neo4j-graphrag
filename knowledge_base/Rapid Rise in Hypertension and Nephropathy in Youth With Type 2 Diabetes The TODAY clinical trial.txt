"Rapid Rise in Hypertension and Nephropathy in Youth With Type 2 Diabetes: The TODAY clinical trial" evaluates the incidence, prevalence, progression, and risk factors associated with hypertension and microalbuminuria among 699 adolescents with youth-onset type 2 diabetes randomized to metformin alone, metformin plus rosiglitazone, or metformin plus an intensive lifestyle intervention. The study found that baseline prevalence of hypertension and microalbuminuria were 11.6% and 6.3%, respectively, and these rates rapidly increased over an average follow-up of 3.9 years to 33.8% for hypertension and 16.6% for microalbuminuria regardless of diabetes treatment group. Multivariate analysis revealed that male sex and higher BMI significantly increased the risk for developing hypertension, whereas higher HbA1c levels were the primary factor significantly associated with an increased risk of developing microalbuminuria. Standardized clinical protocol guidelines implemented during the trial recommended aggressively managing blood pressure and microalbuminuria using early, protocol-driven single or combination pharmacotherapy with ACE inhibitors, along with sodium restriction, caloric reduction, and increased physical activity.
