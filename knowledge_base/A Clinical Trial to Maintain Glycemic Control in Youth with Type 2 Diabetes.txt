"A Clinical Trial to Maintain Glycemic Control in Youth with Type 2 Diabetes (TODAY)” study evaluates the efficacy of three treatment regimens—metformin alone, metformin combined with rosiglitazone, and metformin combined with an intensive lifestyle-intervention program—in achieving durable glycemic control among children and adolescents aged 10 to 17 with recent-onset type 2 diabetes. The study found that 45.6% of participants experienced treatment failure over an average follow-up of 3.86 years, with metformin plus rosiglitazone demonstrating superiority over metformin alone by reducing treatment failure to 38.6% compared to 51.7% for monotherapy, whereas metformin plus lifestyle intervention yielded intermediate results (46.6%) that were not significantly different from metformin alone. Subgroup analyses revealed that metformin plus rosiglitazone was particularly effective in girls, while metformin monotherapy was least effective in non-Hispanic Black participants. Serious adverse events occurred in 19.2% of participants across the trial, and although the paper does not explicitly state formal clinical recommendations or guidelines, it concludes that metformin monotherapy fails to maintain durable glycemic control in roughly half of youth with type 2 diabetes and that adding rosiglitazone provides superior glycemic durability compared to monotherapy or intensive lifestyle interventions.
