The “Cardiovascular Risk Reduction in High-Risk Pediatric Patients A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association Expert Panel on Population and Prevention Science; the Councils on Cardiovascular Disease in the Young, Epidemiology and Prevention, Nutrition, Physical Activity and Metabolism, High Blood Pressure Research, Cardiovascular Nursing, and the Kidney in Heart Disease; and the Interdisciplinary Working Group on Quality of Care and Outcomes Research “ evaluates the evidence surrounding premature cardiovascular disease and accelerated atherosclerosis across eight high-risk pediatric conditions—including familial hypercholesterolemia, diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, post-heart transplantation, Kawasaki disease, congenital heart disease, chronic inflammatory disease, and childhood cancer—to establish targeted cardiovascular risk-reduction strategies. The statement finds that while subclinical atherosclerosis begins in childhood, specific pediatric conditions dramatically accelerate vascular pathology and heighten the risk of premature clinical coronary events, with arterial structural and functional abnormalities correlating directly with risk factor intensity and improving upon risk reduction. To manage these risks, the paper recommends a structured three-tiered risk stratification algorithm (Tier I High Risk, Tier II Moderate Risk, and Tier III At Risk) based on the primary diagnosis and the presence of two or more additional cardiovascular comorbidities. The guidelines mandate comprehensive risk factor screening (evaluating fasting lipids, blood pressure, body mass index, fasting glucose, smoking history, and physical activity) for all high-risk patients, establish universal initial management through therapeutic lifestyle changes focusing on diet and physical activity, treat Tier I conditions as coronary heart disease equivalents requiring immediate disease-specific management, and set tier-specific clinical target cutoffs with guidelines for initiating pharmacologic drug therapy when lifestyle goals are not met.
