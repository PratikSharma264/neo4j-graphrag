"Transfer from paediatric to adult care for young adults with Type 2 diabetes: the SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth Study" evaluates the factors associated with healthcare transfer and poor glycaemic control among young adults with Type 2 diabetes using data from the SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth study. The findings indicate that out of 182 participants, 56% transferred to adult care, 15% received no care, and 29% remained in pediatric care, with higher age at diagnosis and longer diabetes duration predicting the departure from pediatric care. Furthermore, transferring to adult care or receiving no care was significantly associated with higher odds of poor glycaemic control (HbA1c ≥ 9.0%), independent of sex, race, ethnicity, or baseline HbA1c levels. Recommendations and guidelines provided in the paper emphasize the urgent need for tailored clinical programs, healthcare system policies, targeted treatments, patient-centered care, and early education for both adult specialists and generalists to support the growing population of young adults with youth-onset Type 2 diabetes and prevent disease deterioration during the transition period.
