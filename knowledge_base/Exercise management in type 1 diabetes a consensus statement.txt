Exercise management in type 1 diabetes: a consensus statement evaluates the neuroendocrine and metabolic responses to different modalities of physical activity—including aerobic, anaerobic, resistance, and high-intensity interval training—in individuals living with type 1 diabetes. The paper finds that while regular exercise offers substantial cardiometabolic benefits such as improved HbA1c, reduced cardiovascular risk, and enhanced fitness, blood glucose responses fluctuate widely depending on the type of activity, with aerobic exercise typically decreasing blood glucose and anaerobic or high-intensity exercise often causing glucose stability or transient hyperglycemia. To mitigate exercise barriers such as fear of hypoglycemia, the authors present guidelines recommending a target of 150 minutes of weekly physical activity for adults and 60 minutes daily for youth, alongside specific starting blood glucose targets (7–10 mmol/L for aerobic exercise lasting up to an hour, and 5–7 mmol/L for anaerobic or interval training). Furthermore, the statement provides practical recommendations on nutritional intake, insulin dose reductions before and after activity, and continuous glucose monitoring strategies to prevent exercise-related glucose excursions and nocturnal hypoglycemia.
