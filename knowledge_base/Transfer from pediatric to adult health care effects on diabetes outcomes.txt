"Transfer from pediatric to adult health care: effects on diabetes outcomes" evaluates the impact of transitioning from pediatric to adult diabetes care on emerging adults with type 1 diabetes, focusing on outcomes such as diabetes care visit attendance, glycemic control, and acute diabetes-related complications while assessing the methodological strength of existing observational and interventional studies. The paper's findings indicate that while observational studies often report a decline in diabetes care visit frequency alongside an improvement or no change in hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) after transfer, these results are heavily limited by methodological flaws, including incomplete data, high dropout rates among patients who drop out of follow-up, lack of appropriate comparison groups, and confounding physical and psychosocial maturation. Furthermore, studies tracking patients who left the pediatric health system for a broad array of adult providers found deterioration in glycemic control compared to peers remaining in pediatric care, and structured transition interventions showed temporary benefits in clinic attendance or HbA1c that were often not sustained long-term. Regarding recommendations and guidelines, the paper does not propose new clinical guidelines or structured transition protocols; instead, it highlights a critical lack of methodologically strong empirical evidence—noting that most current recommendations are based on expert consensus or clinical experience—and emphasizes that future research must address these shortcomings by utilizing proper comparison groups, tracking patients across diverse adult care settings, and accounting for patient attrition to properly guide clinicians and improve emerging adult care.
