“Early adversity, psychopathology, and latent class profiles of global physical health from preschool through early adolescence” evaluates the longitudinal trajectories of physical health in a sample of 296 children spanning ten years—from preschool through early adolescence (ages 3–13)—and examines whether early psychosocial adversity, family income-to-needs ratio, and preschool-onset psychiatric disorders predict these trajectories and if psychiatric disorders mediate these relationships. Using growth mixture modeling, the study identified two primary trajectories of physical health problems: a stable, low-problem group (n = 199) and a high, increasing group (n = 57) that experienced linear increases in physical health issues over time. The findings reveal that lower early family income-to-needs ratios, higher psychosocial adversity, and early-onset psychiatric diagnoses significantly predicted membership in the high, increasing physical health problem trajectory, with preschool psychiatric disorders partially mediating the link between both types of adversity and worsening physical health outcomes. Based on these findings, the paper highlights the critical need for early clinical identification and intervention targeting both financial/psychosocial risk factors and psychiatric disorders in preschool-aged children to help alter adverse physical health trajectories before they become firmly established.
