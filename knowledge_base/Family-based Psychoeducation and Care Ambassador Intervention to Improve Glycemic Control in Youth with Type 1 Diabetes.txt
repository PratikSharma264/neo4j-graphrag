Family-based Psychoeducation and Care Ambassador Intervention to Improve Glycemic Control in Youth with Type 1 Diabetes: A Randomized Trial evaluates the impact of a 2-year randomized clinical trial testing three levels of care—standard care with basic care coordination, monthly outreach by a Care Ambassador (CA), and monthly CA outreach plus quarterly family-based psychoeducation—on glycemic control (A1C), parental involvement, family conflict, and quality of life among 153 youth with type 1 diabetes. The findings revealed no overall significant differences in A1C across the three groups at the end of two years, and monthly outreach alone provided no added benefit over standard care when regular quarterly clinic visits were maintained; however, a subgroup analysis of youth with suboptimal baseline control (A1C ≥ 8%) showed that the psychoeducational intervention significantly helped maintain or improve A1C levels (77% vs. 52%) and sustain parent involvement (36% vs. 11%) compared to the combined non-psychoeducation groups, all without increasing diabetes-specific family conflict or decreasing youth quality of life. Based on these findings, the authors implicitly recommend incorporating structured psychoeducational strategies—focused on teamwork, setting realistic expectations, and joint problem-solving—alongside routine care coordination specifically for youth with suboptimal glycemic control, while cautioning against relying solely on frequent phone or email outreach without targeted behavioral support
