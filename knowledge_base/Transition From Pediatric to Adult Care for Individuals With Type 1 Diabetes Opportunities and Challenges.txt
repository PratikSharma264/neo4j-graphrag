"Transition From Pediatric to Adult Care for Individuals With Type 1 Diabetes: Opportunities and Challenges" evaluates the barriers, clinical interventions, and transition-of-care strategies for adolescents and young adults with type 1 diabetes as they move from pediatric to adult healthcare settings. The findings indicate that the transition period is marked by worsened glycemic control, increased hospitalization rates, heightened diabetes distress, and significant racial and socioeconomic disparities, often exacerbated by a loss of parental support, lapses in health insurance, and difficulties with self-management like obtaining testing supplies and managing continuous glucose monitors. The paper outlines that while comprehensive transition models show promise in improving clinic attendance and reducing hospitalizations, formal guidelines and standard recommendations remain lacking. Recommendations and guidelines highlighted in the text—largely drawn from organizations like the American Diabetes Association and Got Transition—include starting transition preparation at least one year before the planned transfer, providing a written patient summary to adult endocrinologists, ensuring the first adult visit occurs within 3 to 4 months of the final pediatric visit, and leading critical discussions with young adults around carbohydrate counseling, sleep hygiene, reproductive planning, and diabetes self-reliability.
