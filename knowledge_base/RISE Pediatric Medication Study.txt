The study "RISE Pediatric Medication Study" evaluates two distinct therapeutic approaches—3 months of insulin glargine followed by 9 months of metformin versus 12 months of metformin alone—in preserving or improving β-cell function in youth aged 10–19 years with impaired glucose tolerance (IGT) or recently diagnosed type 2 diabetes during active treatment and 3 months after therapy withdrawal. The main findings indicate that neither treatment regimen significantly improved or preserved clamp-measured β-cell function, as both groups demonstrated progressive deterioration in β-cell responses at 12 and 15 months compared to baseline, with no significant differences observed between the two groups regarding β-cell function, HbA1c, fasting glucose, or oral glucose tolerance test outcomes. Based on these findings, the authors recommend that alternative therapeutic approaches and strategies beyond metformin and insulin glargine be developed to preserve β-cell function and halt disease progression in youth with dysglycemia.
