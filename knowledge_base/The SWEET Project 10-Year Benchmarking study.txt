The SWEET Project 10-Year Benchmarking study evaluates time trends of key quality indicators and the impact of treatment changes in 16,082 youth with type 1 diabetes across 22 centers in 19 countries between 2008–2010 and 2016–2018. Over the 10-year period, adjusted mean HbA1c significantly improved from 68 mmol/mol (8.4%) to 63 mmol/mol (7.9%), coinciding with an increase in insulin pump use from 25.7% to 41.8% and continuous glucose monitoring sensor use from 1.5% to 44.6%. The percentage of patients achieving the target HbA1c of <7% (<53 mmol/mol) rose from 21% in 2008 to 34% in 2018, while severe hypoglycemia rates dropped from 3.8% to 2.4%, and secondary cardiovascular risk factors such as BMI-standard deviation score improved. The findings suggest that twice-yearly international benchmarking, standardized quality control, continuous data monitoring, and the adoption of modern diabetes technologies within centers of reference are effective strategies for improving long-term metabolic outcomes in pediatric type 1 diabetes care.
