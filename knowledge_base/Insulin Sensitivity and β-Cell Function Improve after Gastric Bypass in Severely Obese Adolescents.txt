"Insulin Sensitivity and β-Cell Function Improve after Gastric Bypass in Severely Obese Adolescents" evaluates the metabolic impacts of laparoscopic Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB) on glucose tolerance, insulin sensitivity, and β-cell function in non-diabetic, severely obese adolescents and young adults over a one-year follow-up period. The findings reveal that RYGB resulted in a 38% decrease in body mass index (BMI), a 54% reduction in fasting glucose, a 63% decline in fasting insulin, a 300% increase in insulin sensitivity, and a nearly two-fold improvement in the disposition index (a measure of β-cell function), accompanied by a 21% reduction in the proinsulin-to-C-peptide ratio. While the paper does not specify explicit clinical practice guidelines or formal recommendations, it concludes that RYGB delivers profound early and sustained metabolic benefits by enhancing both insulin sensitivity and β-cell function in severely obese youth, even when significant obesity persists post-surgery.
