"A Randomized, Controlled Trial of Liraglutide for Adolescents with Obesity" evaluates the efficacy and safety of subcutaneous liraglutide (3.0 mg) once daily compared to placebo, both as adjuncts to lifestyle therapy, over a 56-week treatment period followed by a 26-week off-treatment follow-up period in pubertal adolescents aged 12 to under 18 years with obesity and a poor response to lifestyle therapy alone. The study found that liraglutide was superior to placebo in reducing the body-mass index (BMI) standard-deviation score at 56 weeks, with a significantly higher proportion of participants achieving at least a 5% and 10% reduction in BMI, as well as greater reductions in overall BMI, body weight, and waist circumference; however, weight regain was observed after treatment discontinuation, and gastrointestinal adverse events (such as nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea) along with treatment discontinuations were notably higher in the liraglutide group. The paper does not explicitly detail clinical practice guidelines or formal recommendations, though it notes that pharmacologic options like liraglutide offer a valuable adjunct to lifestyle therapy for weight management in adolescents with obesity where treatment options are otherwise limited.
