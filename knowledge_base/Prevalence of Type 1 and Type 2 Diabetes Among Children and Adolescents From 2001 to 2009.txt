“Prevalence of Type 1 and Type 2 Diabetes Among Children and Adolescents From 2001 to 2009” evaluates the changes in the prevalence of physician-diagnosed type 1 and type 2 diabetes among US youth aged 0 to 19 years across sex, age, and race/ethnicity groups between 2001 and 2009 using multi-center surveillance data from the SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth study. The study found that after adjusting for completeness of ascertainment, the overall prevalence of type 1 diabetes increased by 21.1% across all demographic subgroups except American Indians and children aged 0 to 4 years, while the overall prevalence of type 2 diabetes among adolescents aged 10 to 19 years increased by 30.5%, with significant rises observed among white, black, and Hispanic youth. The authors highlight that further studies are required to determine the underlying causes of these increases and note the importance of understanding these population trends to inform clinicians about the medical care required for the growing pediatric population living with diabetes.
