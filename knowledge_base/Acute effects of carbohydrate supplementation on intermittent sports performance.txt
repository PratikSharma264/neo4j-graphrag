Acute effects of carbohydrate supplementation on intermittent sports performance evaluates the biological mechanisms and performance outcomes of consuming carbohydrates during intermittent high-intensity team sports lasting roughly 1 to 2 hours. Drawing on energy system demands that rely heavily on muscle glycogen and blood glucose, the review finds that carbohydrate ingestion generally enhances high-intensity running capacity, delays fatigue, and helps maintain sport-specific technical skills towards the end of prolonged play. Based on these findings, the paper recommends carbohydrate intake during prolonged intermittent exertion to preserve plasma glucose oxidation and central nervous system function, providing guidelines that favor consuming 30 to 60 grams of carbohydrate per hour—or utilizing carbohydrate mouth rinsing for shorter high-intensity sessions—to optimize overall athletic performance.
