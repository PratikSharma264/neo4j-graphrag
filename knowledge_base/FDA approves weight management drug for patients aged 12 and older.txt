"FDA approves weight management drug for patients aged 12 and older" evaluates the safety and efficacy of Saxenda (liraglutide) as an adjunct to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity for chronic weight management in pediatric patients aged 12 and older with obesity weighing more than 60 kg (132 pounds). In a 56-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving 251 pediatric patients, treatment with Saxenda resulted in a 0.23 reduction in BMI standard deviation score (BMI SDS) compared to no reduction in the placebo group, along with an average 2.65% body weight loss versus a 2.37% weight gain in the placebo group. The primary adverse reactions included gastrointestinal side effects, dizziness, fever, and low blood sugar (hypoglycemia in 15% of Saxenda patients versus 4% in placebo). The approval guidelines state that Saxenda should not be combined with other liraglutide products or GLP-1 receptor agonists, its use for pediatric patients with type 2 diabetes has not been established, and prescribing recommendations include monitoring for depression, suicidal thoughts, acute gallbladder disease, heart rate increases, kidney impairment, hypersensitivity, and discontinuing treatment if pancreatitis is suspected due to warnings regarding thyroid C-cell tumors and other risks.
