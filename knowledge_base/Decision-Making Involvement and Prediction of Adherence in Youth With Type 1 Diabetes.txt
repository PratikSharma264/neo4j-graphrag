The study Decision-Making Involvement and Prediction of Adherence in Youth With Type 1 Diabetes: A Cohort Sequential Study evaluates developmental trajectories of decision-making involvement (DMI)—defined as the ways in which parents and children engage each other regarding T1D management—and examines how DMI impacts adherence levels and age-related changes over time in youth aged 8 to 16 assessed five times over two years. Findings revealed that most DMI aspects increased with age, though certain child-reported subscales (Parent Express, Child Seek, and Joint) peaked around ages 12–14 before declining; additionally, higher levels of Child Seek, Child Express, and Joint behaviors were significantly associated with higher overall treatment adherence in both parent and youth reports, though DMI did not significantly alter the rate of adherence decline over time. Recommendations and guidelines suggest targeting interventions toward encouraging proactive child participation in T1D discussions—specifically coaching youth to share information, express opinions, and solicit parental guidance—to support effective self-management as children transition toward greater autonomy.
