The study “Efficacy and safety of atorvastatin in children and adolescents with familial hypercholesterolemia or severe hyperlipidemia: a multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled trial” evaluates the safety and efficacy of atorvastatin (10 mg to 20 mg daily) compared to a placebo over 26 weeks, followed by an additional 26-week open-label phase, in pediatric subjects aged 10 to 17 years with familial hypercholesterolemia or severe hypercholesterolemia. The findings demonstrate that 12 months of treatment with atorvastatin was well-tolerated and resulted in statistically significant reductions in LDL cholesterol (-40% vs. -0.4%), total cholesterol (-32% vs. -1.5%), triglycerides (-12% vs. +1.0%), and apolipoprotein B (-34% vs. +0.7%), alongside a significantly greater increase in HDL cholesterol (+2.8% vs. -1.8%) compared to placebo. Based on these results, the authors conclude that atorvastatin is an effective and safe treatment modality for controlling elevated lipid levels in children and adolescents with severe hypercholesterolemia or familial hypercholesterolemia.
