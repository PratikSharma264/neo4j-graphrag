This study, titled Electronically Verified Use of Internet-Based, Multimedia Decision Aids by Adolescents With Type 1 Diabetes and Their Caregivers evaluates the actual, electronically tracked utilization of web-based decision aids by adolescents with type 1 diabetes and their caregivers considering an insulin pump or continuous glucose monitor within a randomized controlled trial. Findings indicate that 80% of caregivers and 66% of adolescents logged into a decision aid at least once, spending an average of 55.0 and 46.2 minutes on the website and viewing 72.2% and 77.4% of the content, respectively. Bivariate and regression analyses revealed that non-Hispanic, Caucasian females from higher socioeconomic status households were significantly more likely to log in, while adolescent males with lower health literacy demonstrated fewer logins. Based on these results, the paper recommends that future shared decision-making trials seek to actively promote decision aid engagement—particularly among non-White adolescents, potentially via direct assistance during the initial login—and explicitly guidelines that trials employing electronic decision aids routinely measure, report, and analyze objective utilization data.
