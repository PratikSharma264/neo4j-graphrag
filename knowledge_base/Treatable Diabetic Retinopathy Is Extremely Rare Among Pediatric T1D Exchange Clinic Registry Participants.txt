The “Treatable Diabetic Retinopathy Is Extremely Rare Among Pediatric T1D Exchange Clinic Registry Participants” study evaluates the frequency of treatable diabetic retinopathy (DR) among 12,535 pediatric registry participants under 21 years of age with type 1 diabetes, comparing actual cases against existing American Diabetes Association guidelines that recommend annual eye exams starting at puberty or age 10 once diabetes duration reaches 3–5 years. The authors found that while 45 subjects (0.36%) self-reported DR treatment, verified medical and treatment records revealed that none (0%) had actually received treatment for DR. Based on these findings, the paper notes that age- and duration-based screening offers negligible yield at significant financial cost, suggesting that guidelines could be liberalized and refined to include specific risk factors—such as glycemic control history, microalbuminuria, hypertension, and dyslipidemia—thereby allowing resources to be reallocated toward mental health and nutrition counseling, though formal longitudinal studies using retinal photography are required before making firm recommendations.
