The study Impact of dietary protein on postprandial glycaemic control and insulin requirements in type 1 diabetes: a systematic review determines the impact of dietary protein on postprandial blood glucose levels and mealtime insulin requirements in individuals with Type 1 diabetes. Findings indicate that protein consumed alone does not exhibit a significant glycaemic effect until doses reach ≥75 g, whereas within a carbohydrate-containing meal, as little as ≥12.5 g of protein affects postprandial glucose. The timing of this glucose-raising effect ranges from 90 to 240 minutes, and combining fat with a high-protein meal further enhances the postprandial glycaemic response and increases total insulin requirements. Based on these insights, recommendations and guidelines advise that dietary protein must be explicitly taken into account alongside carbohydrates when calculating mealtime insulin doses, emphasizing the need for modified, multi-macronutrient insulin dosing strategies to prevent postprandial hyperglycaemia.
