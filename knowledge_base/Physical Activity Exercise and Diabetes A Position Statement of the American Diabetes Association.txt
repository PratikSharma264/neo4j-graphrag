Physical Activity/Exercise and Diabetes: A Position Statement of the American Diabetes Association evaluates the evidence and clinical strategies regarding the role of physical activity and exercise in managing and preventing type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes, gestational diabetes, and prediabetes. It finds that regular physical activity improves insulin sensitivity, cardiorespiratory fitness, blood glucose control, and vascular health while lowering cardiovascular mortality risk in type 2 diabetes, whereas interrupting sedentary time reduces acute postprandial glucose spikes. The statement recommends that all adults, particularly those with type 2 diabetes, minimize prolonged sedentary behavior by stepping away from sitting every 30 minutes, and that adults with type 2 diabetes perform a combination of aerobic and resistance exercises without letting more than two consecutive days elapse between sessions. To prevent or delay type 2 diabetes onset in high-risk individuals, it recommends lifestyle interventions incorporating at least 150 minutes per week of physical activity and dietary modifications targeting 5% to 7% weight loss. Finally, for individual safety during physical activity, the guidelines specify checking blood glucose prior to exercise to maintain a target starting range between 90 mg/dL and 250 mg/dL, consuming 15–30 grams of fast-acting carbohydrates if glucose is under 90 mg/dL, and testing for ketones—avoiding exercise if moderate-to-large ketone levels are present—when blood glucose exceeds 250 mg/dL.
