Accuracy of Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) during Continuous and High-Intensity Interval Exercise in Patients with Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus evaluates the accuracy of continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) compared to capillary blood glucose measurements during continuous exercise (CON) and high-intensity interval exercise (HIIE) matched at three different standardized mean workloads in individuals with type 1 diabetes mellitus. The findings reveal that CGM sensors generally overestimate capillary blood glucose levels, particularly during low-intensity continuous exercise and low- or high-intensity interval exercise, but demonstrate clinically acceptable overall performance across most conditions except during high-intensity continuous exercise. Based on these results, the paper recommends using CGM to help avoid exercise-induced hypoglycemia during physical activity, but provides the guideline that standard capillary blood glucose monitoring should still be performed alongside CGM during high-intensity exercise to ensure safety and prevent unmanaged glycemic events.
