The “Prevalence of and Risk Factors for Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy in Youth With Type 1 and Type 2 Diabetes: SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth Study“ evaluates the prevalence of and risk factors for diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) among youth with type 1 and type 2 diabetes having a disease duration of at least five years. The study found a DPN prevalence of 7% in youth with type 1 diabetes and 22% in youth with type 2 diabetes. For participants with type 1 diabetes, risk factors associated with DPN included older age, longer diabetes duration, smoking, elevated diastolic blood pressure, obesity, increased LDL cholesterol and triglycerides, lower HDL cholesterol, and poorer long-term glycemic control over time. In youth with type 2 diabetes, DPN was associated with older age, male sex, longer diabetes duration, smoking, and lower HDL cholesterol, with youth with type 2 diabetes having nearly three times higher odds of DPN compared to those with type 1 diabetes even after adjusting for key covariates. Based on these findings, the study highlights a critical need for early screening, improved risk factor management, and targeted interventions in youth aimed at optimizing glycemic control and managing dyslipidemia to prevent or delay debilitating neuropathic complications.
