The study Time in Range for Closed-Loop Systems versus Standard of Care during Physical Exercise in People with Type 1 Diabetes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis evaluates the comparison of time in range (70–180 mg/dL or 3.9–10.0 mmol/L) between fully closed-loop systems and standard of care during physical exercise in people with type 1 diabetes across six randomized controlled trials involving 153 participants (analyzed as 266 participants across 12 comparisons due to crossover designs). The findings reveal that closed-loop systems moderately improved overall time in range during exercise compared to standard of care with an absolute mean difference of 6.18%, with a particularly pronounced and significant benefit in children and adolescents (absolute mean difference of 9.46%) compared to adults (absolute mean difference of 1.07%). Additionally, physical exercise performed on a cycle ergometer demonstrated beneficial effects of closed-loop systems on time in range (mean difference of 12.34%), whereas treadmill exercise and skiing sessions did not show a statistically significant difference. The authors note recommendations highlighting the importance of physical activity for health benefits and mention position statement guidelines concerning glycemic management around exercise—pointing out that continuous glucose monitor accuracy can deteriorate during acute exercise due to physiological lag time between interstitial and blood glucose—and conclude that while closed-loop systems effectively support glycemic control during exercise, further advancement in sensor technology and algorithms is needed to manage rapid glucose fluctuations.
