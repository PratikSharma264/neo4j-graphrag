The “TODAY (Treatment Options for Type 2 Diabetes in Adolescents and Youth) study “ evaluates the frequency of islet cell autoimmunity—specifically GAD-65 and IA-2 autoantibodies—and its associated clinical and laboratory findings in youth aged 10 to 17 years clinically diagnosed with type 2 diabetes. Among the 1,206 screened subjects with a type 2 diabetes phenotype, 9.8% tested positive for islet autoantibodies (5.9% positive for a single antibody and 3.9% positive for both), with antibody positivity being significantly more common in white and male participants. Although antibody-positive youth had significantly lower median BMI, blood pressure, triglycerides, and fasting C-peptide levels, as well as higher HDL cholesterol, their individual physical and metabolic parameters overlapped substantially with those of antibody-negative youth. Based on these findings, the paper indicates that without islet autoantibody testing, clinical and physical characteristics alone cannot reliably distinguish between obese young individuals with type 2 diabetes and those with autoimmune diabetes, highlighting the role of islet cell antibody screening in identifying underlying beta-cell autoimmunity in youth presenting with a type 2 diabetes phenotype.
