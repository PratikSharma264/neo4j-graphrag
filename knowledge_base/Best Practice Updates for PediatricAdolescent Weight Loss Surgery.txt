"Best Practice Updates for Pediatric/Adolescent Weight Loss Surgery" evaluates evidence-based best practice guidelines for pediatric and adolescent weight loss surgery (WLS) based on a systematic search of English-language literature published between April 2004 and May 2007 in PubMed, MEDLINE, and the Cochrane Library. The review finds that while early evidence of safety and efficacy exists for gastric bypass and laparoscopic adjustable gastric banding, current evidence remains insufficient to definitively determine which specific WLS procedures are optimal for adolescents; however, mature adolescent WLS can reduce the risk of early mortality and morbidity from obesity-related diseases. The study recommends modifying patient selection criteria to include adolescents with a BMI ≥ 35 who have specific obesity-related comorbidities showing clear evidence of short-term morbidity (such as type 2 diabetes, severe steatohepatitis, pseudotumor cerebri, or moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea), as well as considering WLS for adolescents with extreme obesity (BMI ≥ 40) and other comorbidities associated with long-term risks. Furthermore, key guidelines emphasized for ensuring patient safety include establishing carefully designed patient selection criteria, conducting multidisciplinary evaluations, choosing appropriate procedures, performing thorough screening and management of comorbidities, optimizing long-term compliance, and obtaining age-appropriate, fully informed consent.
