The clinical report Promoting Optimal Development: Screening for Mental Health, Emotional, and Behavioral Problems evaluates the increasing prevalence of mental health, emotional, and behavioral (MEB) issues among children and outlines updated guidance for pediatric primary care clinicians to effectively screen, identify, and manage these conditions. The report notes that 13% to 20% of U.S. children have a diagnosed MEB disorder—with ADHD, anxiety, disruptive behavior, and mood disorders being the most common—and highlights rising suicide rates alongside exacerbated post-pandemic mental health distress. To address significant gaps in detection and a severe shortage of subspecialist clinicians, the guidelines recommend starting caregiver-focused screening within the first month of life for postpartum depression, followed by child-focused MEB screening at 6, 12, 24, and 36 months, alternating with developmental and autism screenings, and continuing annually after age 3. Additionally, the clinical report provides recommendations on using standardized, culturally responsive screening tools, managing positive screens within the pediatric medical home, promoting family resilience, and overcoming practice-level barriers to streamline workflow and improve care integration.
