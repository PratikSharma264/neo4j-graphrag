The “Health-Related Quality of Life of Children and Adolescents with Type 1 or Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus” evaluates the associations between demographic and diabetes management variables and the health-related quality of life (HRQOL) among 2,445 children and adolescents aged 8 to 22 years with type 1 or type 2 diabetes mellitus across multiple U.S. study sites. The study found that youth with type 2 diabetes reported significantly lower overall HRQOL compared to those with type 1 diabetes; among participants with type 1 diabetes, worse HRQOL was associated with government-funded insurance, insulin injections versus pump therapy, elevated HbA_1c levels (≥9%), higher rates of comorbidities, and recent emergency department visits or hospitalizations, alongside a significant age-by-sex interaction where HRQOL declined in older girls but improved in older boys. For participants with type 2 diabetes, injecting insulin at least three times daily was associated with better HRQOL, whereas having two or more emergency department visits in the preceding six months was associated with lower HRQOL. The authors recommend that interventions aimed at improving HRQOL in youth with diabetes account for treatment modal differences, complication risks, and gender-specific adjustment patterns across developmental age groups.
