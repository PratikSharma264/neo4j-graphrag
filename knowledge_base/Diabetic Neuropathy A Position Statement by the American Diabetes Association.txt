“Diabetic Neuropathy: A Position Statement by the American Diabetes Association” evaluates the epidemiology, classification, diagnosis, prevention, and management of diabetic neuropathies, focusing primarily on distal symmetric polyneuropathy (DSPN) and cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy (CAN). The paper finds that early, optimized glycemic control dramatically reduces the incidence of DSPN and CAN in individuals with type 1 diabetes, whereas glycemic control alone yields more modest or inconsistent benefits in type 2 diabetes, where a multifactorial lifestyle intervention targeting overall cardiovascular risk factors is significantly more effective. Furthermore, the findings highlight that up to 50% of peripheral neuropathies are asymptomatic, placing patients at high risk for foot ulceration, Charcot neuroarthropathy, and amputation if left undetected. Based on these insights, the guidelines recommend screening all patients for DSPN annually—starting at the diagnosis of type 2 diabetes and five years after diagnosis for type 1 diabetes—using a careful history and clinical tests for small-fiber function (pinprick or temperature sensation) and large-fiber function (128-Hz tuning fork and 10-g monofilament testing), while reserving electrophysiological testing for atypical presentations. For neuropathic pain management, the guidelines recommend pregabalin or duloxetine as initial pharmacological treatments, consider gabapentin as an effective alternative, advise caution with tricyclic antidepressants due to side effects, and explicitly advise against using opioids as first- or second-line agents.
