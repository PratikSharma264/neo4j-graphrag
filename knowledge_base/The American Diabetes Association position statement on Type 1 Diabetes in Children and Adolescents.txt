The American Diabetes Association position statement on Type 1 Diabetes in Children and Adolescents emphasizes that pediatric-onset diabetes requires distinct clinical strategies because children are not simply small adults. The disease differs significantly from adult diabetes in its epidemiology, pathophysiology, developmental impact, and response to therapy, meaning care must continuously adapt to match a child's evolving growth stages. The statement outlines a standardized three-stage continuum for disease progression, starting with Stage 1 (presymptomatic with multiple islet autoantibodies and normoglycemia), moving to Stage 2 (presymptomatic with autoantibodies and dysglycemia), and concluding with Stage 3 (symptomatic clinical onset due to insulin deficiency). To manage the condition effectively, diagnosis must be pursued expeditiously to prevent rapid clinical deterioration and diabetic ketoacidosis. Clinicians are advised to differentiate Type 1 diabetes from Type 2, monogenic forms, and atypical presentations using autoantibody panels, family history, and C-peptide testing, while consulting pediatric endocrinologists in ambiguous situations such as stress hyperglycemia. For blood glucose management, the guidelines strongly recommend intensive insulin regimens delivered via multiple daily injections or continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (pumps). Across pediatric age groups, the recommended target A1C level is set at under 7.5%, though this should be tailored based on individual risks like hypoglycemia. Finally, comprehensive care requires monitoring blood glucose multiple times daily, leveraging continuous glucose monitoring systems, and testing blood ketones during acute illness or unexpected hyperglycemia.
