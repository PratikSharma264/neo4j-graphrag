The study titled Cardiorespiratory Fitness and Physical Activity in Pediatric Diabetes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis evaluates the magnitude, precision, and constancy of differences in cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF) and daily physical activity levels between children/adolescents aged 18 years or younger with type 1 diabetes (T1D) or type 2 diabetes (T2D) and their peers without diabetes. Across a systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies, the authors found that cardiorespiratory fitness was significantly lower in youth with T2D (44.5% lower; SMD −1.06) and T1D (10% lower; SMD −0.39) compared to controls, with the deficits in CRF being much larger and more consistent among those with T2D. Daily physical activity was found to be marginally lower in youth with T1D (8.3% to 10% lower; SMD −0.29) and not significantly different in youth with T2D overall (SMD −0.56), though restricting analysis to strictly objective measurement tools revealed significantly reduced physical activity levels in both T2D (SMD −0.71) and T1D (SMD −0.67) groups compared to controls. Based on these findings, the paper highlights an increased risk for future cardiovascular disease-related morbidity in youth with diabetes—particularly those with T2D—and recommends the development of novel behavioral interventions designed to empower affected youth to engage in regular physical activity and improve their cardiorespiratory fitness.
