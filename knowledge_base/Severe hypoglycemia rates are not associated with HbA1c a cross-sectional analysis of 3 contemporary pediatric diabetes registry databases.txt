The study, Severe hypoglycemia rates are not associated with HbA1c: a cross-sectional analysis of 3 contemporary pediatric diabetes registry databases, evaluates the association between glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) levels and severe hypoglycemia rates in pediatric patients with type 1 diabetes receiving usual care by analyzing 12-month observation data (2011–2012) across three registries: the US Type 1 Diabetes Exchange (T1DX), German/Austrian Diabetes Patienten Verlaufsdokumenation (DPV), and Western Australian Children Diabetes Database (WACDD). The authors found that severe hypoglycemia rates per 100 patient-years were 7.1 in T1DX, 3.3 in DPV, and 6.7 in WACDD, and lower HbA1c was not associated with an increased rate of severe hypoglycemia when examined by source registry, treatment regimen, or age group. Based on these findings, the study recommends further investigation in other large, longitudinal cohorts to better characterize the contemporary relationship between glycemic control and severe hypoglycemia risk in pediatric type 1 diabetes patients.
