Disturbed Eating Behavior and Omission of Insulin in Adolescents Receiving Intensified Insulin Treatment: A nationwide population-based study evaluates the prevalence of disturbed eating behavior (DEB) and insulin omission among 770 children and adolescents aged 11–19 with type 1 diabetes using the Diabetes Eating Problem Survey–Revised (DEPS-R). The study found that 27.7% of females and 8.6% of males scored above the cutoff for DEB, with prevalence increasing significantly with both age (8.1% in 11–13 year-olds to 38.1% in 17–19 year-olds) and weight status (7.2% in underweight to 32.7% in obese participants). Additionally, 31.6% of participants reported restricting insulin and 6.9% reported complete insulin omission after overeating. Both DEB and insulin restriction were significantly associated with poorer metabolic control, as evidenced by higher HbA1c levels (9.2% vs 8.4% for DEB; 9.0% vs 8.3% for restriction). The authors suggest that because early intervention is crucial to prevent the development of severe eating disorders and long-term diabetes complications, screening tools like the DEPS-R should use lower cutoff thresholds to identify at-risk youth early for further clinical assessment.
