The study Obesity in youth with type 1 diabetes in Germany, Austria, and the United States examined the prevalence of obesity among youth with type 1 diabetes (T1D) using data from two large pediatric clinical registries in the United States and Europe—the T1D Exchange (n = 11,435) and the Diabetes Prospective Follow-up (n = 21,501). Comparing participants aged 2 to under 18 years against national and international body mass index (BMI) references, researchers found that median BMI values in both registries exceeded standard reference values, indicating that excessive weight is a widespread issue among children with T1D, particularly in the U.S. While no significant BMI differences were found among children aged 2–5 years across the registries, US participants in older age groups (6–17 years) exhibited significantly higher BMI z-scores (P < 0.001). Furthermore, higher BMI z-scores were significantly associated with elevated hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) levels and a higher frequency of severe hypoglycemia across both groups, suggesting that obesity presents additional challenges to achieving optimal glycemic control in young individuals with type 1 diabetes.
