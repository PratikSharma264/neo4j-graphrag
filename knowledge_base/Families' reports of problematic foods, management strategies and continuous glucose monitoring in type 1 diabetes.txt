Families' reports of problematic foods, management strategies and continuous glucose monitoring in type 1 diabetes: A cross-sectional study evaluates the specific foods causing troublesome postprandial blood glucose levels in youth with type 1 diabetes, the management techniques families adopt to control these glycemic spikes, and the influence of continuous glucose monitoring on dietary strategies. In terms of findings, 91% of surveyed families identified problematic high-carbohydrate and high-fat/protein foods—most commonly pizza (60%), pasta (55%), and rice (31%)—and utilized management strategies such as checking blood glucose more frequently (51%), administering a combination bolus (39%), or increasing meal insulin doses by 10% to 25% (32%), while continuous glucose monitoring users demonstrated increased awareness of glycemic impacts (88%), leading over a quarter to adjust their dietary management or restrict certain foods. For guidelines and recommendations, the paper advises that clinicians proactively discuss these specific problematic foods with patients and deliver targeted clinical strategies—such as customizing insulin dosing and delivery patterns—to help families effectively mitigate postprandial hyperglycemia.
