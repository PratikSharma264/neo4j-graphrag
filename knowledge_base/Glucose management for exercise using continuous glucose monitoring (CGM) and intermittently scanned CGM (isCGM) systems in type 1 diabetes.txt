The position statement titled Glucose management for exercise using continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) and intermittently scanned CGM (isCGM) systems in type 1 diabetes evaluates the efficacy, accuracy, and practical clinical application of continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) and intermittently scanned CGM (isCGM) systems around exercise (before, during, and after) in individuals with type 1 diabetes across different age groups and activity levels. The paper finds that while CGM and isCGM technologies significantly reduce time spent in hypoglycemia and improve overall glycemic control, exercise introduces potential sensor inaccuracies and increased lag time (up to 12–24 minutes or longer) between interstitial and blood glucose levels, yielding an overall mean absolute relative difference (MARD) of approximately 13.63% across exercise types. Based on these findings, the paper recommends tailored glucose target thresholds for initiating carbohydrate consumption and exercise—differentiated by exercise intensity, individual hypoglycemia risk, and age—and provides guidelines on optimizing sensor alert settings (e.g., setting lower hypoglycemia alert thresholds up to 5.6 mmol/L / 100 mg/dL at exercise onset to offset sensor lag), using trend arrows to adjust carbohydrate intake or insulin delivery, and performing confirmatory fingerstick blood glucose tests whenever sensor readings conflict with physical symptoms.
