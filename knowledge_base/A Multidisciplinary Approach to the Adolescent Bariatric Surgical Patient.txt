"A Multidisciplinary Approach to the Adolescent Bariatric Surgical Patient" evaluates the establishment, early outcomes, and multidisciplinary framework of a pediatric bariatric surgery program—specifically the Comprehensive Weight Management Center at Cincinnati Children's Hospital Medical Center—for treating clinically severe obesity in adolescents. Examining referrals and initial surgical cases using Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGBP), the study found that RYGBP leads to significant, clinically meaningful weight loss and the reversal of severe comorbidities such as type 2 diabetes and obstructive sleep apnea, though medical and behavioral complications can occur. The paper recommends managing adolescent bariatric surgery within a comprehensive, multidisciplinary team of pediatric specialists to address the unique medical, developmental, and psychosocial needs of young patients. Furthermore, it outlines clinical guidelines requiring candidates to have achieved most of their linear growth, have a BMI ≥40 with severe comorbidities or a BMI ≥50 with less severe comorbidities, and demonstrate the ability to commit to lifelong medical surveillance and dietary supplementation.
