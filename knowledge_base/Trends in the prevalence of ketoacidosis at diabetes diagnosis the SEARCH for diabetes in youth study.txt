The “Trends in the prevalence of ketoacidosis at diabetes diagnosis: the SEARCH for diabetes in youth study“ evaluates temporal changes in the prevalence of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) at the time of diabetes diagnosis among youth aged 0 to 19 years, along with associated risk factors, across three time periods between 2002 and 2010. The study found that DKA prevalence at diagnosis among youth with type 1 diabetes remained persistently high and stable over time at approximately one-third of cases (30.2% in 2002–2003 to 31.1% in 2008–2010), with higher risk associated with younger age, ethnic minority status, lower household income, and lack of private health insurance. In contrast, DKA prevalence among youth with type 2 diabetes decreased significantly from 11.7% in 2002–2003 to 5.7% in 2008–2010 (an approximately 10% annual decline), with higher risk associated with younger age, male gender, and minority race/ethnicity. Based on these findings, the paper highlights a persistent need for increased awareness of diabetes signs and symptoms among the public and healthcare providers, alongside improved access to healthcare, to reduce DKA occurrence at onset in youth with type 1 diabetes.
