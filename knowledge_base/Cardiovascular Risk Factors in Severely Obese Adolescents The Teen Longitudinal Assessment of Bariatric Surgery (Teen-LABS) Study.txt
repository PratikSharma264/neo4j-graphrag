"Cardiovascular Risk Factors in Severely Obese Adolescents: The Teen Longitudinal Assessment of Bariatric Surgery (Teen-LABS) Study" evaluates the preoperative prevalence and predictors of cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk factors among severely obese adolescents undergoing weight-loss surgery across five centers in the United States. Analyzing a cohort of 242 participants, the study found a high baseline prevalence of CVD risk factors, including fasting hyperinsulinemia (74%), elevated high-sensitivity C-reactive protein levels (75%), dyslipidemia (50%), elevated blood pressure (49%), impaired fasting glucose levels (26%), and diabetes mellitus (14%). Furthermore, increased body mass index (BMI) significantly correlated with a higher risk of impaired fasting glucose, elevated blood pressure, and elevated high-sensitivity C-reactive protein levels, while male sex was associated with a greater likelihood of dyslipidemia and elevated blood pressure. Based on these findings, the study highlights that even among severely obese adolescents, the recognition and targeted clinical management of specific CVD risk factors are crucial to help limit the further progression of cardiovascular disease.
