Evidence-Based Behavioral Interventions to Promote Diabetes Management in Children, Adolescents, and Families evaluates contemporary psychological and behavioral interventions designed to support self-management, adherence, and health outcomes among youth with type 1 diabetes (T1D) and type 2 diabetes (T2D) and their families within key theoretical frameworks like Social Cognitive Theory, Family Systems Theory, and Social Ecological Models. Findings demonstrate that structured behavioral approaches—such as skills training, family-based teamwork, behavioral family systems therapy, and multi-systemic therapies—yield modest yet clinically meaningful improvements in regimen adherence, quality of life, and glycemic control, particularly when delivered early post-diagnosis, incorporating family collaboration, or targeting modifiable emotional and family processes rather than relying solely on education. Recommendations highlight tailoring behavioral interventions to meet the unique strengths and needs of individual families, evaluating cost-effectiveness for broader translation, combining mobile and digital health technologies with human contact, and conducting further culturally tailored research for youth with T2D. Finally, guidelines set by national and international diabetes organizations call for routine psychological screening and referral to evidence-based mental health therapies as standard components of pediatric clinical diabetes care.
