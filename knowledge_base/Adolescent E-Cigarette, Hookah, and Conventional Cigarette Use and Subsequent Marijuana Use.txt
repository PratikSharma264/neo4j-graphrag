"Adolescent E-Cigarette, Hookah, and Conventional Cigarette Use and Subsequent Marijuana Use" evaluates whether adolescent electronic cigarette, hookah, or combustible cigarette use is prospectively associated with initiating marijuana use, currently using marijuana, and concurrently using both tobacco and marijuana over a 24-month period among high school students in Los Angeles who had never used marijuana at baseline. The study found that baseline ever-use of e-cigarettes (OR 3.63 for initiation; OR 3.67 for current use) and hookah (OR 3.55 for initiation; OR 4.10 for current use) significantly increased the odds of both initiating and currently using marijuana two years later, showing risk levels comparable to conventional combustible cigarette smoking. Furthermore, adolescents using any of these tobacco products at baseline had more than double the odds of dual tobacco and marijuana use at the 24-month follow-up. Based on these findings, the authors emphasize that policy efforts aimed at protecting adolescent health must account for non-cigarette tobacco products like e-cigarettes and hookah, recommending that public health models evaluating the net impact of alternative tobacco products explicitly incorporate the heightened risk of subsequent youth marijuana adoption.
