The “NEJM 2012 study on estimating glomerular filtration rate (GFR) from serum creatinine and cystatin C” evaluates the accuracy and clinical utility of new GFR estimating equations using standardized serum cystatin C alone and in combination with standardized serum creatinine across diverse populations. The study finds that while equations based on creatinine alone or cystatin C alone show similar bias and accuracy across estimated GFR ranges, the combined creatinine–cystatin C equation significantly improves precision and reduces estimation errors greater than 30%. Furthermore, in participants with a creatinine-based estimated GFR of 45 to 74 mL/min/1.73 m², the combined equation markedly improves the reclassification of measured GFR, correctly reclassifying 16.9% of those with a creatinine-based estimated GFR of 45 to 59 mL/min/1.73 m² as having a GFR of 60 mL/min/1.73 m² or higher. Based on these findings, the authors conclude that the combined creatinine–cystatin C equation performs superiorly to equations using either marker alone and recommend its use as a confirmatory test for chronic kidney disease.
