"Bariatric Surgery for Severely Obese Adolescents" evaluates the 20-year outcomes and safety of bariatric surgery in severely obese adolescents aged 12 to less than 18 years who met the 1991 National Institutes of Health adult eligibility criteria. The study analyzed 33 adolescent patients undergoing various procedures—including gastroplasty and standard, long-limb, or distal gastric bypass—and found that the operations resulted in significant, long-lasting weight loss alongside the resolution or marked improvement of major obesity-related comorbidities such as type 2 diabetes, sleep apnea, hypertension, pseudotumor cerebri, and gastroesophageal reflux disease, while also substantially enhancing self-image and socialization without impairing physical or sexual maturation. Based on these findings, the paper supports offering bariatric surgery to severely obese adolescents who meet adult eligibility criteria, particularly when non-surgical treatments have failed, and recommends considering surgical intervention to prevent long-term health risks and social stigmatization despite current insurance coverage obstacles rooted in outdated consensus guidelines.
