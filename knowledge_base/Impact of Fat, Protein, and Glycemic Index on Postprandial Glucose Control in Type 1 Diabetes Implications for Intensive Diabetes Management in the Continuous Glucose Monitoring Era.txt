Impact of Fat, Protein, and Glycemic Index on Postprandial Glucose Control in Type 1 Diabetes: Implications for Intensive Diabetes Management in the Continuous Glucose Monitoring Era evaluates how dietary fat, protein, and glycemic index affect short-term blood sugar levels after meals in individuals with type 1 diabetes, while also examining the effectiveness of various mealtime insulin dosing strategies for these macronutrients. The review finds that fat, protein, and glycemic index significantly alter postprandial blood glucose levels independently of carbohydrate intake; specifically, fat delays gastric emptying to lower early glucose levels but leads to sustained late hyperglycemia, protein causes delayed blood sugar spikes several hours post-meal, and low glycemic index foods reduce overall glycemic exposure but increase early hypoglycemia risk under standard insulin ratios. Consequently, the authors recommend moving beyond strict carbohydrate counting toward a comprehensive meal-composition approach to insulin dosing that incorporates individualized adjustments and utilizes extended or dual-wave bolus pump features for high-fat or high-protein meals. Additionally, the study highlights American Diabetes Association guidelines recommending formal patient education on the postprandial effects of fat and protein, standard dosing guidelines that advise administering rapid-acting insulin 15 to 20 minutes prior to meals, and the clinical need for evolving practice guidelines that integrate validated dosing algorithms accounting for full macronutrient profiles alongside continuous glucose monitoring technology.
