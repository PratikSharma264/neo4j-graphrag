The study “Risk of diabetic retinopathy at first screen in children at 12 and 13 years of age” evaluates the relationships between age at diagnosis of diabetes, age at diabetic eye screening, and the severity of diabetic retinopathy at the first and subsequent screenings in children aged 12 or 13 years. Analyzing data from 2,125 children across screening programs in England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland, the findings reveal that children diagnosed with diabetes at age 2 or younger had a 20% and 11% proportion of retinopathy at age 12 and 13 respectively, which decreased to 8% and 2% for those diagnosed between ages 2 and 12, with only three children aged 8, 10, and 11 years at diagnosis having images graded with referable retinopathy. Over a mean follow-up of 3.1 years, referable retinopathy developed at a low incidence rate of 4.7 per 1,000 person-years. Based on these findings, the authors conclude with the recommendation that diabetic retinopathy screening earlier than age 12 is not necessary.
