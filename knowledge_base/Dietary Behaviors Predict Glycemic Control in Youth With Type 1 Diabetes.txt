Dietary Behaviors Predict Glycemic Control in Youth With Type 1 Diabetes evaluates the association between dietary adherence and glycemic control among youth with type 1 diabetes. In a cross-sectional study of 119 youth aged 9 to 14 years, researchers measured dietary adherence using the Diabetes Self-Management Profile (DSMP) diet subscale and assessed glycemic control via A1C levels. The study found a statistically significant inverse relationship between dietary adherence scores and A1C levels, with dietary adherence uniquely accounting for 8% of the variance in A1C. Youth in the lowest tertile of parent-reported dietary adherence exhibited an adjusted mean A1C level of 9.0%, which was 0.6% to 0.9% higher than youth in the middle (8.1%) and upper (8.4%) tertiles. Additionally, higher dietary adherence, more frequent blood glucose monitoring, insulin pump use, and higher parent education levels were independently associated with lower A1C levels. The paper notes that dietary adherence was particularly critical for youth on fixed injection regimens of three or fewer daily injections to help manage postprandial glucose fluctuations. Based on these findings, the paper suggests that dietary adherence serves as a key modifiable factor in diabetes management and recommends that future clinical interventions focus on strategies to improve diabetes-specific dietary adherence to help achieve target glycemic control in pediatric populations.
