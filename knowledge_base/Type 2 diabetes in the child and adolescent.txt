"Type 2 diabetes in the child and adolescent" evaluates the clinical presentation, diagnosis, initial and subsequent management, and screening for comorbidities of youth-onset type 2 diabetes. The paper finds that type 2 diabetes in youth occurs predominantly during puberty with a median age of 13.5 years, rarely occurs prepubertally, and rapidly declines in beta-cell function. It notes that while autoantibodies indicate type 1 diabetes, approximately 10–20% of clinically diagnosed pediatric type 2 diabetes cases present with islet autoimmunity, and many youth present with associated comorbidities like hypertension and dyslipidemia at diagnosis. The guidelines recommend using American Diabetes Association criteria for diagnosis, considering routine diabetes autoantibody testing in pediatric clinical type 2 cases, and initiating immediate lifestyle modifications alongside pharmacologic treatment. For metabolically stable patients (HbA1c < 9% with no symptoms), metformin monotherapy titrated up to 2000 mg daily is recommended, whereas basal insulin (0.25–0.5 units/kg/day) with or without metformin is recommended for symptomatic or metabolically unstable patients, aiming for a target HbA1c of <6.5%. Additionally, the paper provides guidelines to screen annually for urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, blood pressure, dyslipidemia, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and retinopathy, initiating targeted therapies such as ACE inhibitors for persistent hypertension or elevated albuminuria, and statins or fibrates for severe dyslipidemia.
