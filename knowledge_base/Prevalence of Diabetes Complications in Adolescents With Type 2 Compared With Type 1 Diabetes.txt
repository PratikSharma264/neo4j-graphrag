"Prevalence of Diabetes Complications in Adolescents With Type 2 Compared With Type 1 Diabetes" evaluates the prevalence of diabetes complications—including retinopathy, microalbuminuria, and peripheral and autonomic neuropathy—along with associated risk factors in 1,433 youth with type 1 diabetes compared to 68 youth with type 2 diabetes aged 18 years or younger in New South Wales, Australia. The study found that while retinopathy was significantly more common in patients with type 1 diabetes (20% vs. 4%), microalbuminuria (28% vs. 6%) and hypertension (36% vs. 16%) were markedly higher in those with type 2 diabetes, despite type 2 patients having a substantially shorter diabetes duration (median 1.3 vs. 6.8 years) and lower median HbA1c levels (7.3% vs. 8.5%). Rates of peripheral and autonomic neuropathy were similar between the two groups, and higher HbA1c was identified as the key significant predictor for microalbuminuria in type 2 diabetes. Based on these findings, the authors recommend early screening for complications and aggressive targeting of glycemic control in pediatric patients diagnosed with type 2 diabetes.
