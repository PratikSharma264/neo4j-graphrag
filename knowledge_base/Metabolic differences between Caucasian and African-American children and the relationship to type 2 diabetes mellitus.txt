The study “Metabolic differences between Caucasian and African-American children and the relationship to type 2 diabetes mellitus” evaluates the metabolic disparities between African-American and white American youth to understand the disproportionately higher rates of type 2 diabetes mellitus among minority pediatric populations. The study finds that African-American children exhibit higher rates of hyperinsulinemia, obesity, family history of diabetes, and acanthosis nigricans, and notably lack a compensatory increase in insulin secretion to offset the decline in insulin action during puberty that is typically observed in white children. Regarding treatment, the paper recommends focusing on identifying at-risk individuals early and instituting targeted lifestyle modifications to mitigate risk factors and prevent the development of type 2 diabetes.
