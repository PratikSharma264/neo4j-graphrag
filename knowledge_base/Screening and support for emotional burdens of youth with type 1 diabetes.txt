Screening and support for emotional burdens of youth with type 1 diabetes: Strategies for diabetes care providers evaluates the psychological and emotional challenges—such as depression, diabetes distress, fear of hypoglycemia, and family conflict—experienced by youth with type 1 diabetes (T1D) and their families, while providing practical framework strategies for diabetes care teams to integrate screening and behavioral support into routine clinical encounters. The authors find that emotional burdens significantly degrade glycemic control, treatment adherence, and overall quality of life, with youth from low socioeconomic backgrounds, single-parent homes, and minority groups facing heightened vulnerability. To address this, the paper recommends utilizing validated, brief screening tools (such as the PHQ-9, GAD-7, or MY-Q) alongside multidisciplinary collaboration with pediatric psychologists and mental health professionals to deliver manageable, in-clinic behavioral strategies like goal-setting, problem-solving, and motivational communication. Furthermore, it highlights guidelines from the American Diabetes Association (ADA) and the International Society for Pediatric and Adolescent Diabetes (ISPAD), which explicitly call for routine psychosocial assessments at diagnosis and on an annual basis to evaluate diabetes distress, quality of life, disordered eating, and family dynamics.
