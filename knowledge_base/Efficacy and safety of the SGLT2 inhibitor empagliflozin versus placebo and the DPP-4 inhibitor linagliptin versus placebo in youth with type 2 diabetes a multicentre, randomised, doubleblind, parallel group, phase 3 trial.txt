"Efficacy and safety of the SGLT2 inhibitor empagliflozin versus placebo and the DPP-4 inhibitor linagliptin versus placebo in youth with type 2 diabetes: a multicentre, randomised, doubleblind, parallel group, phase 3 trial" evaluates the efficacy and safety of the SGLT2 inhibitor empagliflozin (10 mg or 25 mg daily) and the DPP-4 inhibitor linagliptin (5 mg daily) compared to a shared placebo over 26 weeks in youth aged 10–17 years with type 2 diabetes who were previously treated with metformin and/or insulin. The study found that empagliflozin provided a statistically significant and clinically relevant reduction in HbA1c baseline levels compared to placebo (-0.84%, p=0.012), whereas linagliptin did not show a statistically significant reduction (-0.34%, p=0.29). Hypoglycemia was the most frequently reported adverse event, with higher rates observed in active treatment groups, though no severe hypoglycemia cases occurred. Based on these findings, the paper suggests that empagliflozin may offer a valuable new oral treatment option for children and adolescents with type 2 diabetes to address the therapeutic unmet need in this population.
