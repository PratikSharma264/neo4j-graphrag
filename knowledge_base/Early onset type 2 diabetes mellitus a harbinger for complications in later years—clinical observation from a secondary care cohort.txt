"Early onset type 2 diabetes mellitus: a harbinger for complications in later years—clinical observation from a secondary care cohort" evaluates the magnitude of diabetes complications, the adequacy of risk factor management, and the role of diabetes duration in a cross-sectional secondary care cohort of 2,733 subjects categorized by diagnosis age before or after 40 years. The study found that by the sixth decade of life, the early-onset cohort experienced a high complication burden—including cardiovascular disease (37.2%), retinopathy (59.3%), and neuropathy (53.1%)—reaching a similar microvascular complication rate approximately 13 to 20 years earlier than those diagnosed later in life. Furthermore, diabetes duration significantly predicted microvascular and cardiovascular complications, and early-onset individuals exhibited poorer glycemic control (70–78%), untreated hypertension (26.3%), and underutilization of statin primary prevention (34.8%) despite an overall cardiovascular risk factor prevalence of 80–93%. The paper notes that prolonged exposure to an adverse diabetic milieu drives this accelerated complication rate and emphasizes that current management of cardiovascular and glycemic risk factors in early-onset patients remains inadequate.
