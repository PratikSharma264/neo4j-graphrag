“Drug Therapy of High-Risk Lipid Abnormalities in Children and Adolescents” evaluates the association between pediatric lipid abnormalities and early atherosclerosis, reviews limitations in existing pediatric screening guidelines, and examines clinical trial evidence regarding the efficacy and safety of lipid-lowering drug therapies in youth with severe or familial hypercholesterolemia. The paper finds that the atherosclerotic process begins during childhood, progresses faster when compounded by high low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol and additional cardiovascular risk factors, and that statin therapy can safely and effectively reverse early vascular structural and functional abnormalities in young patients. Based on these findings, the guidelines recommend targeted screening using family history and fasting lipid profiles, with initial management prioritizing six to twelve months of dietary and lifestyle modifications. The recommendations advise initiating lipid-lowering drug therapy primarily in children aged 10 and older who maintain persistent LDL cholesterol levels of 190 mg/dL or higher, or 160 mg/dL or higher if accompanied by multiple risk factors or a family history of premature cardiovascular disease, alongside routine clinical and laboratory monitoring.
