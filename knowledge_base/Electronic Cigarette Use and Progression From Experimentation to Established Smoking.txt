"Electronic Cigarette Use and Progression From Experimentation to Established Smoking" evaluates the prospective association between baseline electronic cigarette use and subsequent progression to established cigarette smoking among 1,295 adolescent cigarette experimenters aged 12 to 17 using nationally representative data from the Population Assessment of Tobacco and Health (PATH) survey across a one-year follow-up period. The study finds that ever using e-cigarettes was positively and independently associated with progression to current established smoking (adjusted OR: 1.80; 95% CI: 1.04–3.12), while positive associations for smoking at least 100 cigarettes and past 30-day smoking remained elevated but fell just short of statistical significance in fully adjusted models. Additionally, baseline past 30-day e-cigarette use significantly predicted future past 30-day cigarette smoking, and former e-cigarette use predicted current established smoking. Based on these findings, the authors conclude that e-cigarettes do not divert youth from conventional smoking and may instead encourage progression to regular combustible cigarette use, highlighting recommendations for regulatory actions, such as local restrictions on flavored tobacco products and careful federal oversight of e-cigarette availability, to reduce youth appeal and protect public health.
