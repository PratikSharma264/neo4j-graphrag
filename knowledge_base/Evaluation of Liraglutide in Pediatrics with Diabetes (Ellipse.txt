"Evaluation of Liraglutide in Pediatrics with Diabetes (Ellipse)" evaluates the efficacy and safety of subcutaneous liraglutide (up to 1.8 mg per day) added to metformin (with or without basal insulin) compared to placebo in children and adolescents aged 10 to less than 17 years with type 2 diabetes over a 26-week double-blind period followed by a 26-week open-label extension. The study found that at 26 weeks, the mean glycated hemoglobin level decreased by 0.64 percentage points in the liraglutide group while increasing by 0.42 percentage points in the placebo group—yielding an estimated treatment difference of -1.06 percentage points—and this difference widened to -1.30 percentage points by 52 weeks alongside significant reductions in fasting plasma glucose. However, while overall adverse event rates were similar between groups, patients receiving liraglutide experienced a higher frequency of gastrointestinal adverse events, such as nausea and vomiting. The trial concluded that liraglutide effectively improves long-term glycemic control in pediatric patients with type 2 diabetes, though clinicians must consider the trade-off of increased gastrointestinal side effects during treatment.
