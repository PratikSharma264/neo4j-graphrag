"Pediatric Obesity—Assessment, Treatment, and Prevention: An Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline" evaluates evidence-based practices for diagnosing, preventing, and managing pediatric obesity and its associated health risks. The guideline finds that pediatric obesity is a major global health concern driven by genetic susceptibilities in a permissive environment, noting that underlying endocrine etiologies are rare and typically accompanied by impaired growth velocity. To diagnose and screen patients, the guidelines recommend using Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) BMI percentiles for children aged two and older, while recommending against routine insulin testing or general endocrine labs unless height velocity is reduced. For treatment and prevention, the guidelines recommend prescribing family-centered lifestyle modifications that emphasize nutrient-dense diets, limiting screen time to one to two hours per day, and participating in 20 to 60 minutes of daily physical activity. Furthermore, pharmacotherapy is suggested only after intensive lifestyle interventions fail and is restricted primarily to older adolescents, while bariatric surgery is reserved for mature adolescents with severe obesity (BMI ≥40 kg/m², or ≥35 kg/m² with significant comorbidities) within specialized multidisciplinary care settings.
