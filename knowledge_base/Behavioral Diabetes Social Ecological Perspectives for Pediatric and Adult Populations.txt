Behavioral Diabetes: Social Ecological Perspectives for Pediatric and Adult Populations evaluates the complex interaction of psychosocial, behavioral, and ecological factors in managing diabetes across pediatric and adult age groups, emphasizing that optimal glycemic control relies heavily on addressing daily life stressors and psychological well-being alongside standard medical treatment. The text finds that psychosocial stressors significantly impact patient and family adherence to diabetes care routines, making behavioral support a critical component of effective disease management. To improve patient outcomes, it recommends integrating behaviorally oriented specialists into diabetes care teams and addressing the broader social and environmental contexts surrounding individuals with diabetes.
