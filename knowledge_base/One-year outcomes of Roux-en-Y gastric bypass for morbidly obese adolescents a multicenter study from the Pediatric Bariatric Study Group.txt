"One-year outcomes of Roux-en-Y gastric bypass for morbidly obese adolescents: a multicenter study from the Pediatric Bariatric Study Group" evaluates the 1-year weight loss, metabolic changes, and complications following Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGBP) in a multicenter cohort of 36 morbidly obese adolescents (ages 13–21) compared to a nonsurgical control group. The study found that surgical patients experienced a significant 37% drop in mean body mass index (from 56.5 kg/m² to 35.8 kg/m², alongside significant improvements in metabolic variables including triglycerides, total cholesterol, fasting blood glucose, and fasting insulin, whereas the nonsurgical group showed no statistically significant BMI change. While 61% of surgical patients had no complications, 15 patients experienced adverse events, including 9 minor complications, 4 moderate complications, and 2 severe medical complications (including one case of severe beriberi and one late death secondary to C. difficile colitis and multiorgan failure), with no perioperative deaths or severe surgical complications observed. The authors recommend close, multidisciplinary monitoring of adolescent bariatric patients to ensure nutritional compliance, detect complications early, and incorporate targeted interventions like B-complex vitamin supplementation, while highlighting the need for further prospective studies to better define indications, surgical options, and long-term safety guidelines.
