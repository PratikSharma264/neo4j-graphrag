Are children with type 1 diabetes safe at school? Examining parent perceptions evaluates parent perceptions regarding the safety of, and satisfaction with, the diabetes care their children with Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus receive at school. The study compares parents in permissive states—where trained non-medical school personnel are permitted to provide diabetes care—with parents in non-permissive states, where only licensed healthcare professionals can provide such care. The findings indicate that parents in permissive states perceive their children to be just as safe during both the regular school day and extracurricular activities as parents in non-permissive states. Furthermore, parental satisfaction regarding overall diabetes care and specific responses to hypo- or hyperglycemia shows no statistically significant differences across state types. Although nearly all schools maintain a health clinic or nurse's office, non-permissive state schools have full-time registered nurses present more frequently than permissive state schools. During regular hours, care is primarily delivered by school nurses and the students themselves, while parents and students handle the majority of care during extracurricular activities, with over a third of parents reporting that they are required to attend outside-of-school events to manage their child's diabetes. Based on these findings, the study recommends that training non-medical school personnel is an effective strategy to maximize student safety and provide parental peace of mind when a full-time school nurse is unavailable. Most parents specifically recommend that teachers, coaches, and gym teachers receive diabetes care training alongside nurses to ensure comprehensive support. Regarding guidelines, the study emphasizes following established pediatric treatment standards that recommend individualizing blood glucose targets to reduce glucose variability and improve glycemic control throughout the school day. It highlights the importance of maintaining consistent monitoring and prompt intervention for both hypo- and hyperglycemia to minimize cognitive disruption and keep students safe during academic and extracurricular activities.
