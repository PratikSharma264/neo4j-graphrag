"Effects of a Patient-Centered Intervention to Reduce Alcohol Use Among Youth With Chronic Medical Conditions" evaluates the impact of a novel, self-administered, condition-tailored psychoeducational intervention on alcohol use frequency, risk perceptions, and alcohol-related knowledge among youth aged 14 to 18 with chronic medical conditions, including type 1 diabetes, juvenile idiopathic arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, and inflammatory bowel disease. The study found that exposure to the intervention significantly improved alcohol-related knowledge scores and increased risk intolerance compared to controls. Although overall drinking frequency increased over time across the broader sample, gender-stratified analyses revealed that female drinkers in the intervention group significantly reduced their mean frequency of drinking days compared to an increase among control females. Based on these findings, the authors recommend further research to optimize and tailor this patient-centered psychoeducational model so that its health-protective benefits can be effectively extended to both male and female adolescents living with chronic medical conditions.
