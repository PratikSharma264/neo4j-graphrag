Advancing Diabetes Management in Adolescents: Comparative Effectiveness of Mobile Self-Monitoring Blood Glucose Technology and Family-Centered Goal Setting evaluates three strategies—a health information technology (HIT)-enhanced blood glucose meter that shares real-time data and allows text messaging, a clinic-based family-centered goal setting intervention using motivational interviewing, and a combined approach of both—to improve self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) and glycemic control (HbA1c) in adolescents aged 12–18 with type 1 diabetes over a 6-month period. The study found that HIT-enhanced SMBG alone resulted in no change in mean HbA1c, family-centered goal setting showed a non-significant decrease of 0.3% at 6 months, and the combined approach demonstrated a significant HbA1c reduction of 0.6% at 3 months that diminished to a non-significant 0.4% reduction by 6 months, with higher baseline HbA1c levels predicting greater improvement; additionally, there were no significant changes or group differences in the daily frequency of SMBG testing. Based on these findings, the authors recommend further research into pairing family-centered goal setting with HIT-enhanced diabetes technologies, noting that family goal setting and counseling may be particularly useful when adolescents adopt new self-management technologies to achieve optimal clinical outcomes, especially among those with higher baseline HbA1c levels.
