Insulin strategies for dietary fat and protein in type 1 diabetes: A systematic review evaluates the efficacy of various insulin dosing and delivery strategies used to manage postprandial glycaemia following meals high in fat and protein in individuals living with type 1 diabetes. Synthesizing data from 18 randomized controlled trials published between 1995 and 2021, the paper finds that administering additional insulin—specifically an extra 24% to 75% of the insulin-to-carbohydrate ratio (ICR)—improves postprandial glucose control, whereas increases below 24% generally offer little glycaemic benefit. The findings also demonstrate that split or combination boluses outperform standard single doses, and administering insulin 15 minutes prior to the meal yields superior postprandial glycaemic outcomes. Based on these insights, the paper provides supportive guidelines and recommendations for managing high-fat and high-protein meals, advising an additional 24% to 75% ICR delivered via a combination bolus with a minimum upfront dose of 60% ICR, administered 15 minutes before eating.
