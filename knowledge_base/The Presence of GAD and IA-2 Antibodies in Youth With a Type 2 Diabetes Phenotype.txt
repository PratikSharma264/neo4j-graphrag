“The Presence of GAD and IA-2 Antibodies in Youth With a Type 2 Diabetes Phenotype” evaluates the frequency of islet cell autoimmunity—specifically GAD-65 and IA-2 autoantibodies—and its associated clinical and laboratory characteristics in youth aged 10–17 years clinically diagnosed with type 2 diabetes. Among 1,206 screened participants with an overweight or obese phenotype, 9.8% tested positive for islet autoantibodies (5.9% single-antibody positive and 3.9% double-antibody positive), with autoantibody positivity being significantly more frequent in white and male participants. Compared to their autoantibody-negative counterparts, antibody-positive youth displayed less marked characteristics of type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndrome, exhibiting lower BMI z scores, blood pressure, serum triglycerides, and fasting C-peptide levels, as well as higher HDL cholesterol levels and a higher frequency of insulin use. Despite these group-level statistical differences, individual parameters like BMI z score, C-peptide, lipid levels, and blood pressure showed substantial overlap between antibody-positive and antibody-negative groups, demonstrating that physical features and routine clinical measurements alone cannot reliably differentiate obese youth with type 2 diabetes from those with autoimmune-mediated diabetes without formal autoantibody testing.
