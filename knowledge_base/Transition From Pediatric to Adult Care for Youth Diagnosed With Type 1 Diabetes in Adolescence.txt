"Transition From Pediatric to Adult Care for Youth Diagnosed With Type 1 Diabetes in Adolescence" evaluates sociodemographic and clinical correlates of glycemic control and the transition from pediatric to adult medical care among adolescents diagnosed with type 1 diabetes. The study found that the estimated median age of transition to adult care was 20.1 years, with 57% of participants having switched to adult providers by follow-up and 77% having left pediatric care by age 21. Older age, lower baseline glycosylated hemoglobin, and higher parental education were independently associated with increased odds of transitioning. Importantly, transferring from pediatric to adult care was associated with a 2.5-fold increase in the odds of having poor glycemic control (hemoglobin A1c ≥9%) at follow-up, alongside other risk factors such as nonwhite race/ethnicity and higher baseline hemoglobin A1c. Based on these findings, the study highlights that young adults require additional support during their move to adult medical care. Furthermore, referencing guidelines from the American Academy of Pediatrics, the American College of Physicians, the American Academy of Family Practice, and the American Diabetes Association, the paper notes that all young adults with diabetes should transition to adult-oriented medical care between 18 and 21 years of age.
