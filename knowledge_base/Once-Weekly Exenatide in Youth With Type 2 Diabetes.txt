"Once-Weekly Exenatide in Youth With Type 2 Diabetes" evaluates the efficacy and safety of a once-weekly 2 mg exenatide injection compared to a placebo in pediatric patients aged 10 to <18 years with type 2 diabetes suboptimally controlled by current treatments. The study found that at 24 weeks, once-weekly exenatide significantly reduced glycated hemoglobin compared to placebo, showing a least squares mean change of -0.36% for exenatide versus +0.49% for placebo (a between-group difference of -0.85%). Secondary end points showed nonsignificant numerical differences favoring exenatide in fasting plasma glucose, body weight, and systolic blood pressure, while adverse events occurred in 61.0% of participants in the exenatide group versus 73.9% in the placebo group, with no major hypoglycemia events reported in either group. The paper notes that exenatide was well tolerated and received FDA approval as an adjunct to diet and exercise for pediatric patients aged 10 to <18 years with type 2 diabetes based on these results.
