The study on the psychometric properties of the Parent and Child Problem Areas in Diabetes Measures evaluates the reliability and validity of adapted survey instruments (PAID-C for youth ages 8–12 and P-PAID-C for their parents) to measure diabetes-specific emotional distress across a sample of 804 children with type 1 diabetes and 968 parents recruited from 42 U.S. diabetes camps. Factor analyses supported an 11-item, two-factor structure for the child measure (capturing emotional burden and regimen-specific distress) and a 16-item, four-factor second-order structure for the parent measure (capturing negative emotions, keeping up with chronic demands, personal regimen-specific distress, and child regimen-specific distress), with both tools demonstrating high internal consistency and criterion validity through positive correlations with HbA1c and negative correlations with diabetes-related strengths and self-care skills. The authors recommend utilizing these brief, standardized measures for routine, clinic-based screening to enable early identification of diabetes-specific emotional distress, guide clinical decision-making, and inform timely interventions before distress negatively impacts glycemic outcomes or leads to diabetes burnout.
