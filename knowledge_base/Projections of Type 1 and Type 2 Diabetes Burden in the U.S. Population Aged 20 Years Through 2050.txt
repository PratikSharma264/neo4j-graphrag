The study titled “Projections of Type 1 and Type 2 Diabetes Burden in the U.S. Population Aged <20 Years Through 2050” evaluates the future national burden of type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) among U.S. youth under age 20 through 2050 using a dynamic Markov modeling framework that incorporates SEARCH study data and U.S. Census Bureau population projections. Under a baseline scenario with constant incidence rates, the projected number of youth with T1DM increases from 166,018 in 2010 to 203,385 in 2050, while youth with T2DM increases from 20,203 to 30,111, both driven primarily by demographic growth among racial and ethnic minority populations. Under an increased incidence scenario, the number of youth with T1DM nearly triples to 587,488 (a 144% prevalence increase to 5.20/1,000), and T2DM almost quadruples to 84,131 (a 178% prevalence increase to 0.75/1,000), with minority youth—particularly Hispanic and Non-Hispanic Black populations—bearing the highest proportional impact. Based on these findings, the paper recommends urgent public health action to curb these potential increases, calling for additional research into effective T2DM primary prevention strategies aimed at youth obesity and high-risk groups, alongside enhanced health care system planning and expanded medical workforce training to manage the growing pediatric diabetes burden.
