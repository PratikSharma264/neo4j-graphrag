"FDA approves treatment for chronic weight management in pediatric patients aged 12 years and older" evaluates the supplemental indication of Qsymia (phentermine and topiramate extended-release capsules) for chronic weight management in pediatric patients aged 12 years and older with obesity, defined as a BMI at or above the 95th percentile. In a 56-week clinical study of 223 adolescents aged 12 to less than 17, participants taking Qsymia lost an average of 4.8% (7.5 mg/46 mg dose) and 7.1% (15 mg/92 mg dose) of their BMI, compared to a 3.3% BMI gain in the placebo group. The safety findings highlight potential risks including fetal harm (oral clefts), increased heart rate, suicidal ideation, visual problems, and slowing of linear growth, with common pediatric adverse reactions including depression, dizziness, joint pain, fever, flu, and ankle sprains. The guidelines recommend Qsymia as an adjunct therapy to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity when lifestyle intervention alone is unsuccessful. Due to birth defect risks, the recommended REMS program guidelines require patients who can become pregnant to obtain a negative pregnancy test before starting treatment, perform monthly pregnancy testing, and use effective contraception during treatment.
