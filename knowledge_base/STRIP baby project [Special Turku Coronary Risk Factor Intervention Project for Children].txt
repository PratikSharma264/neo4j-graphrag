The “STRIP baby project [Special Turku Coronary Risk Factor Intervention Project for Children] “ evaluates the changes in serum cholesterol ester fatty acid composition during the transition from infant feeding to an adult-type diet in 7- and 13-month-old children subjected to a 6-month low-saturated fat, low-cholesterol dietary intervention. The study found that while milk type (breast milk versus formula) strongly influenced dietary and serum cholesterol ester fatty acid compositions at 7 months of age, the 6-month dietary counselling intervention—which successfully altered the children's actual fat intake quality at 13 months—had only a minimal effect on serum cholesterol ester fatty acid levels, as serum compositions in all 13-month-olds closely resembled those of 7-month-old breastfed infants regardless of intervention or previous milk type. The paper guidelines and recommendations for the intervention group included keeping saturated fat intake to approximately 10% of daily energy after 1 year of age (with 30–35% total energy from fat) as per Nordic Nutrition Recommendations, which was implemented by recommending skimmed milk supplemented with 2 to 3 teaspoonfuls of vegetable oil daily to ensure an adequate intake of total fat and essential fatty acids.
