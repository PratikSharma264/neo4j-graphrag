The “Characteristics of adolescents and youth with recent-onset type 2 diabetes: the TODAY cohort at baseline” evaluates the baseline demographic, clinical, medical history, and biochemical characteristics of 704 American adolescents and youth aged 10 to 17 years who were diagnosed with recent-onset type 2 diabetes for less than two years and had a body mass index at or above the 85th percentile. The findings reveal that the participant group was predominantly female (64.9%), had a mean age of 14.0 years, and consisted mostly of racial and ethnic minority populations, including 41.1% Hispanic and 31.5% non-Hispanic Black youth, with 89.4% having a family history of diabetes and significant socioeconomic challenges such as 41.5% residing in households earning less than $25,000 annually. Furthermore, clinical and biochemical comorbidities were highly prevalent within two years of diagnosis, including low high-density lipoprotein levels in 79.8%, blood pressure at or above the 90th percentile in 26.3%, microalbuminuria in 13.0%, and elevated triglycerides in 10.2% of participants. The study does not explicitly provide formal clinical practice guidelines or recommendations, but it emphasizes that youth-onset type 2 diabetes presents with substantial risk factors and metabolic abnormalities early in the course of the disease.
