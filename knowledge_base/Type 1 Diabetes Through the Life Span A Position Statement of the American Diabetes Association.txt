The study titled Type 1 Diabetes Through the Life Span: A Position Statement of the American Diabetes Association evaluates the global and domestic incidence, diagnostic challenges, and comprehensive management strategies for type 1 diabetes across all age groups. It finds that type 1 diabetes is characterized by autoimmune beta-cell destruction leading to lifelong insulin dependence, affecting millions globally with substantial age-related and geographic variation in incidence. The study recommends recognizing clinical presentation variations between youth and adults, utilizing pancreatic autoantibody panels to differentiate type 1 from type 2 diabetes, and establishing early, structured transition plans from pediatric to adult clinical care teams. Additionally, guidelines provided in the paper outline standardized diagnostic criteria—such as fasting plasma glucose and A1C thresholds—alongside scheduled protocols for initial, quarterly, and annual evaluations covering microvascular and macrovascular complication screenings, autoimmune comorbidity checks, and individualized self-management support across all developmental stages.
