The study Social Determinants of Health and Diabetes: A Scientific Review evaluates the literature on the associations of five key social determinants of health (SDOH)—socioeconomic status, neighborhood and physical environment, food environment, health care, and social context—with diabetes risk and health outcomes, as well as the impact of interventions targeting these factors. Findings indicate that lower income, lower educational attainment, housing instability, food insecurity, and poor healthcare access are consistently associated with higher diabetes prevalence, greater risk of complications, worse glycemic control, and increased mortality, with widening disparities over time. While direct interventions on structural socioeconomic factors remain understudied, literacy-adapted education and health-literacy tools show small but statistically significant improvements in glycemic control (HbA1c). Based on these findings, the paper recommends strengthening linkages between healthcare systems and community-based sectors, expanding diabetes research to evaluate multi-level structural interventions targeting SDOH, and providing evidence-based guidelines to inform clinical practice and policy toward achieving health equity.
