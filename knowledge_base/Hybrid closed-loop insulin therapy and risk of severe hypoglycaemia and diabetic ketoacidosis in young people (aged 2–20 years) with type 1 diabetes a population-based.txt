Hybrid closed-loop insulin therapy and risk of severe hypoglycaemia and diabetic ketoacidosis in young people (aged 2–20 years) with type 1 diabetes: a population-based study evaluates whether rates of severe hypoglycaemia and diabetic ketoacidosis are lower with hybrid closed-loop insulin therapy compared with sensor-augmented open-loop pump therapy in a large cohort of 13,922 young people from 250 diabetes centres across Germany, Austria, Switzerland, and Luxembourg. The study found that individuals using closed-loop therapy had a significantly higher rate of diabetic ketoacidosis (1.74 vs 0.96 per 100 patient-years)—a risk particularly pronounced in those with HbA1c levels of 8.5% or higher—and no overall difference in severe hypoglycaemia rates compared to open-loop users, though they experienced lower rates of hypoglycaemic coma (0.62 vs 0.91 per 100 patient-years), lower overall HbA1c levels, a higher percentage of time in the target glucose range, and reduced glycaemic variability. Based on these findings, the authors recommend additional educational measures, increased alertness, and ketone monitoring when utilizing automated insulin delivery to mitigate the elevated risk of diabetic ketoacidosis.
