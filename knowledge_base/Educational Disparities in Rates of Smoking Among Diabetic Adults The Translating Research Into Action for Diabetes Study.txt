"Educational Disparities in Rates of Smoking Among Diabetic Adults: The Translating Research Into Action for Diabetes Study" evaluates educational disparities in smoking rates among 6,538 diabetic adults aged 25 years or older across multiple managed care health plans in seven US states. The findings reveal an overall smoking prevalence of 15% among participants, with a significant educational gradient that varies markedly across age groups: the gradient is strong among adults aged 25–44 years (with approximately 50% of those with less than a high school education reporting current smoking), modest among those aged 45–64 years, and nonexistent among those aged 65 years or older. Based on these results, the paper recommends targeted public health and managed care interventions for smoking prevention and cessation specifically tailored for young, poorly educated people with diabetes, emphasizing communication techniques appropriate for individuals with low educational attainment and inadequate functional health literacy, as well as the implementation of cost-effective strategies like telephone quit lines and mobile text messaging.
