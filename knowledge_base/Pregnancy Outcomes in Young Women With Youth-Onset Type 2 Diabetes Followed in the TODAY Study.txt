"Pregnancy Outcomes in Young Women With Youth-Onset Type 2 Diabetes Followed in the TODAY Study" evaluates maternal and fetal outcomes among 141 young women with youth-onset type 2 diabetes who experienced a total of 260 pregnancies over a maximum follow-up of 15 years. The study found high rates of maternal and neonatal complications: 65% of women experienced pregnancy complications, 35% had chronic hypertension, 25% had pre-pregnancy nephropathy, and 31.9% maintained elevated HbA1c  levels (≥8%). Regarding outcomes, pregnancy loss occurred in 25.3% of cases and preterm birth in 32.6%, while offspring showed rates of 7.8% small for gestational age, 26.8% large for gestational age, and 17.9% macrosomia. Notably, prior contraception use was reported by only 13.5% of the participants. The authors conclude that these substantial maternal and infant risks—exacerbated by significant socioeconomic disadvantage—could potentially be mitigated through higher rates of contraception use and improved reproductive planning in this population.
