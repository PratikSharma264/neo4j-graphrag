The Diabetes Screening With Hemoglobin A1c Versus Fasting Plasma Glucose in a Multiethnic Middle-School Cohort evaluates middle-school students with glycemic abnormalities by comparing high-risk hemoglobin A1c (hrA1C: 5.7–6.4%) with impaired fasting glucose (IFG: 100–125 mg/dL) to characterize their distribution, durability, and association with other diabetes risk factors. In this multiethnic 6th-grade cohort of 3,980 students, IFG was five times more common than hrA1C (16.0% vs. 3.2%), but hrA1C was more persistent over a two-year period into 8th grade (59.4% vs. 46.9%). High-risk A1C was strongly associated with non-Hispanic Black race/ethnicity, family history of diabetes, higher BMI, waist circumference, and fasting insulin, whereas IFG was associated with Hispanic ethnicity, male sex, higher BMI, waist circumference, fasting insulin, high blood pressure, and mean triglycerides. Over the 2-year study, very few children developed diabetes thresholds (0.8% with hrA1C developed A1C ≥6.5%, and 1.1% with IFG developed FPG ≥126 mg/dL), and only 58% of those identified with diabetes in 8th grade had previous baseline abnormalities. The study notes that optimal screening strategies and guidelines for diabetes in youth remain unresolved due to the modest predictive value of both measures and differing risk profiles.
