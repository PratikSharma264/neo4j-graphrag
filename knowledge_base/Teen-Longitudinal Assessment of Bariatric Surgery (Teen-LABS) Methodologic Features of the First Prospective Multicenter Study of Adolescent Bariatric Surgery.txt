"Teen-Longitudinal Assessment of Bariatric Surgery (Teen-LABS): Methodologic Features of the First Prospective Multicenter Study of Adolescent Bariatric Surgery" evaluates the methodological design and protocol of the first prospective, multicenter observational study designed to collect coordinated clinical, epidemiological, and behavioral data on adolescents (≤19 years old) undergoing bariatric surgery compared to adult historical counterparts. The paper outlines the study findings regarding protocol structure, noting that 200 adolescent patients across four clinical centers and a comparison group of 200 adults across five centers will be tracked to determine baseline differences in health status, evaluate early (30-day) and intermediate-term (1–2 year) surgical risks, and compare psychosocial domains including eating behaviors, depressive symptoms, and health-related quality of life. The paper provides guidelines for ancillary study proposals, specifying that external researchers may utilize Teen-LABS data or biospecimens provided their protocols do not interfere with the observational nature of the core study and receive approval following established submission procedures.
