"Prevalence of Tobacco Use and Association between Cardiometabolic Risk Factors and Cigarette Smoking in Youth with Type 1 or Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus" evaluates the prevalence of tobacco use and its association with cardiometabolic risk factors among youth aged 10 to 22 years with type 1 or type 2 diabetes mellitus using data from the SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth study. The findings demonstrate that tobacco use is prevalent in this population, increasing significantly with age and reaching 34.0% in youth ≥20 years with type 1 diabetes and 40.3% in those with type 2 diabetes, with higher smoking rates among youth from lower-income families (<$50,000). Furthermore, current cigarette smoking in youth with type 1 diabetes was significantly associated with higher odds of high triglyceride levels and physical inactivity, alongside less favorable overall cardiometabolic risk profiles across both diabetes types. Despite these risks, less than half of younger participants (aged 10–14 years) reported ever receiving counseling from a healthcare provider to avoid or quit smoking. Based on these observations, the paper recommends that aggressive tobacco prevention and smoking cessation programs be made a high priority in clinical care to prevent or delay the onset of cardiovascular disease in youth with diabetes mellitus.
