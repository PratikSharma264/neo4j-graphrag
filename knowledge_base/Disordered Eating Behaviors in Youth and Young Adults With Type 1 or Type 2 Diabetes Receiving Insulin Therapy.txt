Disordered Eating Behaviors in Youth and Young Adults With Type 1 or Type 2 Diabetes Receiving Insulin Therapy:The SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth Study evaluates the prevalence of disordered eating behaviors (DEB) and their associations with glycemic control, insulin sensitivity, and psychosocial functioning in youth and young adults with type 1 or type 2 diabetes receiving insulin therapy. Analyzing a cohort of 2,156 participants with type 1 diabetes and 149 participants with type 2 diabetes using the Diabetes Eating Problem Survey–Revised (DEPS-R), the study found that DEB was present in 21.2% of individuals with type 1 diabetes and 50.3% of those with type 2 diabetes. Across both diabetes types, participants experiencing DEB demonstrated higher BMI z scores, lower insulin sensitivity, increased depressive symptoms, and a lower quality of life compared to those without DEB, with diabetic ketoacidosis episodes occurring significantly more often in type 1 diabetes participants with DEB. Based on these findings, the paper recommends heightened clinical awareness and early interventions tailored to address disordered eating in this vulnerable population, alongside calling for future longitudinal studies to track the long-term trajectory of DEB and its impact on diabetes outcomes.
