The study titled "Microvascular complications assessment in adolescents with 2- to 5-yr duration of type 1 diabetes from 1990 to 2006" evaluates the prevalence trends and risk factors of early microvascular complications—specifically retinopathy, nephropathy, and neuropathy—in 819 adolescents aged 11 to 17 years with 2 to 5 years of type 1 diabetes duration treated at a tertiary pediatric clinic over a 17-year period. The study found that early retinopathy declined significantly from 16% to 7% between 1990 and 2002 before plateauing, early indicators of nephropathy (elevated albumin excretion rate and microalbuminuria) remained stable over time, and peripheral nerve abnormalities increased from 14% to 23%, all occurring alongside improvements in median HbA1c and increased daily insulin injections. Based on the presence of early complications even within short diabetes durations, the paper supports clinical guidelines recommending that routine microvascular screening in pubertal children with type 1 diabetes commence after 2 years of disease duration.
