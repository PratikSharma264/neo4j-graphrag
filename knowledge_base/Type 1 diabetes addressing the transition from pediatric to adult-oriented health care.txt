"Type 1 diabetes: addressing the transition from pediatric to adult-oriented health care" evaluates the multiyear process of transitioning adolescents and young adults with type 1 diabetes from pediatric to adult-oriented health care systems, highlighting associated challenges such as increased insulin resistance during puberty, rising hemoglobin A1c levels, and gaps in continuous medical care. The paper's findings indicate that early transition can lead to worse glycemic control, reduced annual clinic visits, and high rates of care lapses, while a gradual transition approach and retention in specialty adult endocrinology care help mitigate these risks. Regarding recommendations and guidelines, professional organizations such as the American Diabetes Association and the American Academy of Pediatrics advise initiating transition preparation at least one year prior to transfer (or as early as ages 12–13), systematically assessing transition readiness using validated tools or diabetes-specific checklists, gradually shifting diabetes management responsibilities from parents to the patient, providing specific adult care provider referrals, and routinely screening for both microvascular/macrovascular complications and psychosocial issues such as depression and disordered eating.
